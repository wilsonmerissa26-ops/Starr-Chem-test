# 0. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

Day 3 teaches the resonance foundation Organic Chemistry expects the learner to use automatically:

1. recognize whether two drawings are true resonance contributors of the **same species**;
2. know what may move and what must stay fixed;
3. move electron pairs with curved arrows without moving atoms;
4. generate a valid new resonance contributor;
5. recompute formal charges after the electron move;
6. preserve electron count, overall charge, and valid second-row valence;
7. distinguish equivalent contributors from unequal contributors; and
8. explain which contributor matters more using structure, not memorized labels.

The Day 3 readiness target comes directly from the existing chemistry-readiness assessment:
- draw a resonance contributor and formal charges for nitrate;
- recognize that moving a hydrogen means the drawings are **not** resonance structures;
- compare contributors using octets, charge placement, and electronegativity; and
- explain contributor importance from memory rather than selecting a memorized phrase.

Day 3 does **not** yet teach acid/base ranking, pKa, reaction mechanisms, aromaticity, or resonance as a reason for acidity. Those later topics may use Day 3 evidence, but they do not belong inside this lesson.

## Accuracy anchors

This contract follows the standard Organic Chemistry rules used by OpenStax:
- curved arrows show movement of electrons, not atoms;
- resonance contributors keep the same atom connectivity;
- second-row atoms may not exceed an octet;
- incomplete-octet contributors such as carbocations can still be valid contributors, but they are usually less important than comparable full-octet contributors;
- nonequivalent contributors contribute unequally, with the resonance hybrid resembling the more stable contributor more strongly;
- equivalent contributors contribute equally; and
- formal charges must still sum to the overall molecular/ionic charge.

Reference anchors:
- OpenStax Organic Chemistry 2.4, **Resonance**
- OpenStax Organic Chemistry 2.5, **Rules for Resonance Forms**
- OpenStax Organic Chemistry 2.6, **Drawing Resonance Forms**
- OpenStax Organic Chemistry 2.3, **Formal Charges**

# 2. VOCABULARY IS DIAGNOSTIC, NOT MASTERY

Day 3 tracks term recognition separately from resonance reasoning.

Terms used today:
- **resonance contributor** — one valid electron-placement drawing of the same species;
- **resonance hybrid** — the real structure represented by the contributors together;
- **delocalized electrons** — electrons represented as spread across more than one atom/bond position;
- **pi bond (π bond)** — the electron pair in the second bond of a double bond that may participate in resonance;
- **curved arrow** — shows where an electron pair starts and where that electron pair goes.

A learner may understand the move while forgetting a word. A vocabulary miss is recorded as a word gap and does not automatically become a resonance-concept failure.

Vocabulary evidence never counts as independent resonance mastery.

# 3. THE NON-NEGOTIABLE RESONANCE IDEA

Teach this before any arrow rule:

> **Resonance moves electrons. It does not move the atoms.**

Two drawings can be resonance contributors only if:
- the same atoms are present;
- the atoms have the same connectivity / sigma-bond skeleton;
- only lone pairs, pi bonds, and the formal charges created by those electron placements change;
- the total number of electrons is preserved; and
- the overall charge of the species is unchanged.

A hydrogen moving from one atom to another is **not resonance**. That changes atom connectivity.

A sigma bond being broken and reattached somewhere else is **not an ordinary resonance move**.

The contributors are not separate molecules flipping back and forth. The real molecule/ion is the resonance hybrid.

## Immediate low-risk interaction

Before curved arrows are introduced, show two pairs of drawings:

**Pair A:** same carbon skeleton, pi bond moves one position, positive charge moves accordingly.

**Pair B:** a hydrogen moves from one atom to another.

Ask:
1. Which pair can be resonance?
2. What stayed fixed in the valid pair?
3. What physically moved in the invalid pair?

The learner must do something with the fixed-skeleton idea before arrow notation appears.

---

# 4. CURVED ARROWS — WHAT THE ARROW ACTUALLY MEANS

Curved arrows move **electron pairs**.

The arrow tail must begin at electrons that actually exist:
- a lone pair; or
- a pi bond.

The arrow head points to where that electron pair will be placed:
- an adjacent atom; or
- an adjacent bond position, creating a new pi bond.

A formal `+` sign is not an electron source. An empty orbital/cationic center may be an electron **destination**, but the tail still must begin at an actual electron pair.

Do not teach arrow drawing as decoration. Every arrow must predict a specific change in the next contributor.

## Three checks after every electron move

1. **Skeleton check:** did any atom or sigma-bond connectivity move? It must not.
2. **Electron/octet check:** were electrons conserved, and did any second-row atom end with **more than eight electrons**? Exceeding the octet is invalid. An incomplete octet can still describe a valid contributor, but it weakens that contributor during ranking.
3. **Charge check:** recompute changed formal charges and make sure their signed sum still equals the species charge.

# 5. TEACHING REPRESENTATIONS

Exact teaching examples are frozen here so independent evidence can stay fresh.

## Teacher example A — allyl cation

Use labeled atoms:

`C1H2 = C2H − C3H2+  ↔  +C1H2 − C2H = C3H2`

Teach:
- C1, C2, and C3 never change positions;
- the original C1=C2 pi-electron pair moves toward the C2−C3 bond;
- the new pi bond is C2=C3;
- the positive formal charge appears on C1 after that electron pair leaves the original pi bond;
- total charge stays +1.

Low-risk interaction:
- learner identifies the electron source before the arrow is shown;
- learner identifies the destination bond before the new contributor is revealed.

## Teacher example B — acetate

Use one displayed acetate contributor only as teaching:

`CH3−C(=Oa)−Ob−  ↔  CH3−C(−Oa−)=Ob`

Teach the paired move:
1. a lone pair on Ob forms the C=Ob pi bond;
2. simultaneously, the original C=Oa pi electrons move onto Oa so carbon never exceeds an octet;
3. the atom skeleton is unchanged;
4. the −1 formal charge moves from Ob to Oa;
5. total charge stays −1;
6. because Oa and Ob are equivalent oxygens in acetate, the two contributors are equivalent and contribute equally.

Do not use acetate itself later as clean independent evidence.

## Teacher example C — carbonyl contributor ranking

Compare:

`R2C=O  ↔  R2C+−O−`

Teach why the neutral carbonyl contributor is more important:
- the neutral contributor gives carbon a complete octet;
- the charge-separated contributor leaves carbon with an incomplete octet; and
- the neutral contributor also avoids charge separation.

Important distinction: the charge-separated form is still a **valid resonance contributor**. It is simply a smaller contributor.

# 6. HOW TO COMPARE CONTRIBUTORS

Apply these questions in order:

1. **Are both drawings valid contributors of the same species?** If atom connectivity changed, stop: they are not resonance contributors.
2. **Does any second-row atom exceed an octet?** If yes, that drawing is invalid.
3. **Do comparable valid contributors give key second-row atoms full octets?** A valid full-octet contributor generally outranks a comparable valid incomplete-octet contributor.
4. **How much charge separation is present?** With comparable octets, fewer separated formal charges is generally better.
5. **Where are the charges?** Negative formal charge is better accommodated on the more electronegative atom; positive charge is better accommodated on the less electronegative atom, all else comparable.
6. **Are the contributors equivalent by symmetry / identical atom environments?** Equivalent contributors contribute equally.

Do not teach “the most symmetric picture wins.” Symmetry matters only when it establishes genuine equivalence.

Do not let a lower-priority rule override an octet comparison without explanation. For the ordinary C/N/O structures used here, full-octet preference is checked before charge-separation/electronegativity tie-breakers.

# 7. GUIDED PRACTICE — TWO FRESH FORMS

Guided work is supported and never counts as clean independent evidence.

## Guided G1 — allyl anion

Structure:

`C1H2 = C2H − C3H2−`

Learner sequence:
1. identify the electron source as the C3 lone pair, not the `−` symbol;
2. identify C2−C3 as the bond where that pair will form a pi bond;
3. identify the existing C1=C2 pi pair that must move so C2 does not exceed an octet;
4. identify C1 as the atom receiving that old pi-electron pair;
5. produce/select `−C1H2 − C2H = C3H2`;
6. identify C1 as the carbon carrying −1 in the new contributor;
7. confirm total charge remains −1.

## Guided G2 — nitrite, NO2−

Show one valid contributor with one N=O and one N−O−. Nitrogen has one lone pair.

Learner sequence:
1. identify the lone-pair source on the singly bonded O−;
2. identify the N−O bond where the new pi bond forms;
3. move the old N=O pi pair onto the other oxygen;
4. assign changed formal charges:
   - N = **0**;
   - double-bonded O = **0**;
   - singly bonded O = **−1**;
5. verify the total remains −1;
6. decide that the two contributors are equivalent because the oxygens exchange identical bonding/charge roles in equivalent positions.

If help is used, Guided stays supported as expected; no mastery penalty is needed because Guided never counted clean in the first place.

# 8. INDEPENDENT BANK — FRESH EVIDENCE

Use up to six independent items. Stop when sufficient evidence is established; do not force all six after the contract is already satisfied.

A clean item requires:
- scaffold level 0;
- no hint, first-step reveal, walkthrough, notebook, or answer-bearing support;
- no prior wrong attempt on that item; and
- a correct explanation when the item asks why.

Any help or wrong attempt converts the current item to supported/repaired practice. The next clean attempt must use a different item.

## I1 — true resonance or not?

In the scored pair, a hydrogen has moved from one carbon to another while the double bond also changes.

Required production:
- **Not resonance**;
- reason: atom/H connectivity changed, not just electron placement.

Tags: `validity`, `connectivity`.

## I2 — substituted allylic anion

Use:

`CH3−CH=CH−CH2−`

Number the three-atom allylic segment as C2=C3−C4−.

Required production:
- identify the lone pair on C4 as the electron source;
- form the new C3=C4 pi bond;
- move the old C2=C3 pi pair onto C2;
- produce `CH3−CH−−CH=CH2` with the **−1 formal charge on C2**;
- total remains −1.

The phrase “other end” must not be used without the C2/C3/C4 labels because the molecule contains four carbons.

Tags: `generate`, `carbon-only`, `charge-conservation`.

## I3 — formate, HCO2−

Show one contributor with one C=O and one C−O−.

Required production:
- generate the other contributor;
- carbon FC = **0**;
- double-bonded oxygen FC = **0**;
- singly bonded oxygen FC = **−1**;
- total = −1;
- state that the two contributors are equivalent because the oxygens exchange identical roles.

Tags: `generate`, `heteroatom`, `equivalent`, `formal-charge`.

## I4 — carbonyl ranking

Use acetone rather than the generic teacher drawing.

Compare:
- neutral C=O contributor;
- charge-separated C+−O− contributor.

Required production:
- identify the neutral contributor as the larger contributor;
- explain that carbon has a full octet in the neutral contributor but an incomplete octet in the charge-separated contributor;
- also note that the neutral contributor avoids charge separation.

Tags: `rank`, `octet`, `charge-separation`.

## I5 — nonadjacent electron move

Show a molecule containing a lone pair that is **not adjacent** to the pi system, plus a proposed arrow that jumps across a saturated atom.

Required production:
- reject the proposed resonance move;
- explain that the source is not in the continuous adjacent conjugated pathway and the proposed move does not create a valid resonance contributor.

Tags: `validity`, `adjacency`, `arrow-source`.

## I6 — nitro-group equivalence

Use `R−N+(=O)−O−` and ask for the contributor where the two oxygens exchange double-bond / negative-charge roles.

Required production:
- valid second contributor;
- N FC = **+1**;
- double-bonded O FC = **0**;
- singly bonded O FC = **−1**;
- overall nitro fragment remains neutral when attached to neutral R;
- contributors are equivalent when the two oxygens are equivalent.

Tags: `generate`, `heteroatom`, `equivalent`, `formal-charge`.

# 10. ERROR DIAGNOSIS

Diagnose from the first visible invalid move, not merely the final drawing.

| Error code | What it means | Targeted correction |
|---|---|---|
| `CONNECTIVITY_CHANGED` | Atom or H moved | Freeze atom labels / sigma skeleton and ask what electron pair can move instead. |
| `SIGMA_BOND_CHANGED` | A sigma bond was broken/reconnected | Highlight the fixed sigma skeleton; resonance changes lone pairs/pi bonds, not atom connectivity. |
| `ARROW_TAIL_EMPTY` | Arrow begins at + charge / empty location | Ask: “Where are the electrons at the tail?” Require a lone pair or pi bond. |
| `NOT_ADJACENT` | Proposed source/destination is not in an adjacent conjugated path | Mark the saturated interruption and return to the nearest adjacent p/lone-pair relationship. |
| `OCTET_EXCEEDED` | Electron move gives a second-row atom more than eight electrons | Count the receiving atom’s bonds/lone pairs before allowing the arrow. |
| `INCOMPLETE_OCTET_RANK` | Valid contributor with an incomplete octet was ranked too highly | Keep it as a valid contributor, then compare it with the full-octet contributor. |
| `ELECTRONS_NOT_CONSERVED` | Electron pair invented/lost | Replay only the arrow pair and account for the pair before/after. |
| `FC_MISMATCH` | Electron move may be right but formal charge is wrong | Keep the contributor and run Day 2 V−N−B only on the changed atom(s). |
| `CHARGE_SUM` | Atom charges do not add to the species charge | Add signed formal charges; do not reteach arrow movement if the move was valid. |
| `EQUIVALENT_MISSED` | Learner ranks equivalent contributors unequally | Map equivalent atom roles side-by-side. |
| `RANK_CHARGE_SEPARATION` | Extra separated charges ignored when octets are comparable | Count formal-charge sites in each contributor. |
| `RANK_ELECTRONEGATIVITY` | Charge is preferred on the less appropriate atom | Compare electronegativity only after validity/octet/charge-separation checks. |

Two repeats of the **same error code** trigger a representation change rather than a third verbal restatement.

Suggested representation switches:
- words → fixed atom-label diagram;
- diagram → electron-pair tokens that physically move;
- tokens → one-arrow Watch demonstration;
- ranking words → side-by-side octet/charge table.

# 11. HELP POLICY

Independent resonance help has the same three learner choices as the locked framework.

## Give me a hint

Point to what to inspect, not the answer.

Examples:
- “Find an electron pair next to the pi system.”
- “Check whether the atom skeleton changed.”
- “Before ranking, compare octets.”

## Give me the first step

Identify only the first decision, then hand it back.

For an arrow problem:
- identify the electron **source category** (lone pair or pi bond), but do not draw the arrow or reveal the resulting contributor.

For a ranking problem:
- ask the learner to compare octets first, without naming the winner.

## Walk me through it

Interactive sequence, one learner response per step:
1. same atom skeleton: yes/no;
2. electron source;
3. electron destination;
4. any second arrow needed to avoid an over-octet;
5. resulting pi bond/lone-pair placement;
6. changed formal charge(s);
7. total charge check;
8. equivalence/ranking decision and why.

A walkthrough must not dump the complete contributor before the learner supplies the intermediate moves.

Any help converts that item to supported practice. A fresh item is required for clean evidence.

# 12. COLD WHOLE-CONCEPT TRANSFER

The transfer combines validity, arrow movement, formal charge, charge conservation, and contributor comparison.

## Transfer T1 — nitrate, NO3−

Show one valid nitrate contributor with:
- one N=O double bond;
- two N−O single bonds.

Required learner production:
1. identify a lone pair on one singly bonded O− as the source;
2. identify that N−O bond as the destination for the new pi bond;
3. identify the old N=O pi pair that must move onto the original double-bond oxygen so N does not exceed an octet;
4. produce a different valid nitrate contributor without moving atoms;
5. assign formal charges:
   - N = **+1**;
   - double-bonded O = **0**;
   - each singly bonded O = **−1**;
6. verify the total = **−1**;
7. state that the three nitrate contributors are equivalent because the three oxygens occupy equivalent positions in nitrate; and
8. explain that the same atoms/sigma skeleton remain while electron placement changes.

T1 is cold. No hint or notebook is visible unless the learner explicitly converts it to practice.

If T1 is wrong or helped, diagnose the first specific failure and use fresh T2.

## Transfer T2 — amide resonance

Use `CH3−C(=O)−NH2`.

Required learner production:
1. identify the nitrogen lone pair as the electron source;
2. form the C=N pi bond;
3. move the original C=O pi pair onto oxygen;
4. produce `CH3−C(−O−)=N+H2`;
5. O FC = **−1**, N FC = **+1**, and total charge = **0**;
6. compare the neutral and charge-separated contributors;
7. identify the neutral amide contributor as the larger of these two valid contributors because both have complete octets but the neutral contributor avoids charge separation; and
8. explain the ranking rather than merely stating “neutral is better.”

T2 must be clean and cold to clear Day 3 after a contaminated T1.
