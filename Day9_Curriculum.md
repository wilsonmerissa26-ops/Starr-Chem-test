# DAY 9 CURRICULUM — INTEGRATED READINESS TRANSFER / CAPSTONE

## 1. Purpose and scope
bounded integration of structure, resonance, acid/base, electron flow, and energy reasoning. This is readiness preparation, not a replacement for Organic Chemistry I.

### Chemistry accuracy anchors
- Capstone evidence must be fresh and unsupported.
- A weak subskill routes to its Review target without restarting all days.
- Coverage spans structure/formal charge, stability, acid/base direction, electron flow, and thermodynamics versus kinetics.

## 2. Explicitly out of scope
untaught mechanisms, reaction taxonomy, synthesis, spectroscopy, and a full Organic Chemistry I course.

## 3. Prior-day handoff and repair
The gate reads Days 4-8 evidence, with surgical Review routing for any weak prerequisite. Missing evidence opens a short prerequisite retrieval. A miss routes only that subskill to Review/Needs review and never resets earlier days.

## 4. Teaching examples (contaminated)
Teacher demonstration TCH-A and visual TCH-V expose the complete reasoning. They may create Seen/Guided evidence only and can never appear in the independent or transfer banks.

## 5. Guided examples
G1 names each reasoning step with prompts. G2 removes the final-answer prompt. Any help preserves the response, diagnoses the error, and contaminates that item.

## 12. Final status and handoff

## 14. Auditable runtime question and answer-key bank

The wording below is the learner-visible production task. Fallbacks are fresh equivalents and never repair the contaminated original.

### D9-G-INTEGRATE — guided

**Question:** For acetate, state formal charge and resonance meaning, then connect that stability to acetic-acid strength.

**Chemistry answer key:** Acetate is −1 and delocalizes charge over two oxygens; stabilizing the conjugate base supports acetic-acid acidity.

**Semantic evidence required:** `-1` or `negative one`; `delocal`; `two oxygen`; `stable`; `conjugate base`; `acid`.

### D9-I-STRUCTURE-ACID — independent

**Question:** Formate HCO2− has two equivalent resonance contributors. State its net charge, what moves, and why formic acid is stronger than methanol.

**Chemistry answer key:** Formate is −1; electrons, not atoms, shift between contributors, delocalizing charge over two oxygens. It is more stable than methoxide, so formic acid is stronger.

**Semantic evidence required:** `-1` or `negative one`; `electron`; `not` or `atom`; `delocal`; `two oxygen`; `more stable`; `formic`; `stronger`.

### D9-F-STRUCTURE — independent fallback

**Question:** Fresh capstone: analyze benzoate charge/resonance and compare benzoic acid with benzyl alcohol.

**Chemistry answer key:** Benzoate is −1 with charge delocalized over two oxygens and is more stable, making benzoic acid stronger.

**Semantic evidence required:** `-1` or `negative one`; `electron`; `delocal`; `two oxygen`; `more stable`; `benzoic`; `stronger`.

### D9-I-EQUILIBRIUM-FLOW — independent

**Question:** CN− + CH3OH ⇌ HCN + CH3O− (pKa 9.2 and 16). Choose equilibrium direction. Separately, for CN− + CH3Br, identify roles and both arrows.

**Chemistry answer key:** The acid-base equilibrium favors reactants containing higher-pKa methanol. In substitution CN− attacks electrophilic methyl carbon as C–Br electrons go to Br−.

**Semantic evidence required:** `left` or `reactant`; `methanol` or `16`; `higher pka`; `weaker acid`; `cn-` or `cyanide`; `nucleophile`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `c-c` or `bond`; `c-br` or `bond`; `bromine` or `br-`.

### D9-F-FLOW — independent fallback

**Question:** Fresh capstone: NH3 + H2O ⇌ NH4+ + OH− (pKa 9.25,15.7); then describe NH3 + CH3I arrows.

**Chemistry answer key:** Equilibrium favors reactants; NH3’s lone pair attacks methyl carbon and C–I electrons go to iodine.

**Semantic evidence required:** `left` or `reactant`; `water` or `15.7`; `higher pka`; `nh3` or `ammonia`; `nucleophile`; `lone pair`; `carbon`; `c-n` or `bond`; `c-i` or `bond`; `iodine`.

### D9-I-ENERGY — independent

**Question:** A favorable substitution has reactants 0, products −25, peak 85 kJ/mol. Give ΔG, barrier, favored side, and likely qualitative rate.

**Chemistry answer key:** ΔG is −25 and products are favored; ΔG‡ is 85, a high barrier consistent with a slow reaction.

**Semantic evidence required:** `-25`; `85`; `activation` or `barrier`; `product` or `favor`; `slow`; `high barrier`.

### D9-F-ENERGY — independent fallback

**Question:** Fresh capstone: reactants 10, products 30, peak 40 kJ/mol. Give ΔG, forward barrier, favored side, and rate implication.

**Chemistry answer key:** ΔG = +20, barrier = 30; reactants are favored while the modest barrier concerns rate.

**Semantic evidence required:** `+20` or `20`; `30`; `activation` or `barrier`; `reactant` or `favor`; `lower` or `small` or `faster`.

### D9-T-INTEGRATED — transfer

**Question:** Phenoxide reacts with CH3Br. Explain why phenoxide is stabilized yet nucleophilic, describe both arrows, and distinguish a negative reaction ΔG from its activation barrier.

**Chemistry answer key:** Resonance stabilizes phenoxide, whose oxygen pair can still attack electrophilic methyl carbon; C–Br electrons leave to Br. Negative ΔG concerns favorability, while ΔG‡ controls rate.

**Semantic evidence required:** `phenoxide`; `resonance` or `delocal`; `nucleophile`; `oxygen` or `lone pair`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `c-o` or `bond`; `c-br` or `bond`; `bromine`; `negative`; `product` or `favor`; `barrier`; `rate`.

### D9-T-INTEGRATED-FALLBACK — transfer fallback

**Question:** Fresh transfer: acetate attacks CH3I. Connect acetate resonance/formal charge, electron-flow arrows, and the meanings of ΔG and ΔG‡.

**Chemistry answer key:** Acetate is −1 and resonance-stabilized; an oxygen pair forms C–O as C–I electrons go to iodine. ΔG gives favorability and ΔG‡ gives rate.

**Semantic evidence required:** `acetate`; `-1` or `negative`; `resonance` or `delocal`; `oxygen` or `lone pair`; `carbon`; `c-o` or `bond`; `c-i` or `bond`; `iodine`; `delta g` or `dg` or `free energy`; `favor`; `barrier`; `rate`.

## 15. Support, retrieval, and routing behavior

The cold prompt shows no definition, choices, word bank, or support visual. Hint identifies the feature to inspect; First step asks for the species/result before its reason; Walkthrough and IDK reveal the complete keyed reasoning and contaminate that encounter. Wrong answers remain on the same problem for bounded repair. A correct contaminated response routes to the named fresh equivalent. Only a first-attempt, unsupported response to an Independent item counts. After supported teach-back, the later fallback is a fresh no-clue retrieval with a reset attempt budget. Notebook records clean versus supported encounters; Review records the misconception code; Summary reports clean evidence and transfer. Missing prerequisite evidence routes to that day’s Review without resetting completed days.
