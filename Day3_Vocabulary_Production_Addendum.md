# DAY 3 VOCABULARY PRODUCTION ADDENDUM

**This addendum supersedes Day3_Curriculum.md Section 2 only. All other Day 3 resonance content and mastery rules remain frozen.**

## Why this changed

Real-device testing exposed a teaching gap: recognition could move the learner forward even when she said she could not explain the word. That is not enough evidence. A learner can select a familiar definition by remembering fragments, using clues, or eliminating choices without being able to retrieve or explain the concept independently.

A second red-team audit then exposed a deeper checker problem: presence-only keyword matching could mistake a contradiction for understanding. For example, a sentence containing “electrons do not move” must not earn credit merely because it contains the words “electrons” and “move.” A bag of clue words such as “same species electron placement” is also not an explanation. Vocabulary grading therefore has to check both the required ideas and any conflicting ideas expressed by the learner.

## Terms

- **resonance contributor**: one valid electron-placement drawing of the same species
- **resonance hybrid**: the real structure represented by the contributors together
- **delocalized electrons**: electrons spread across more than one atom or bond position instead of being confined to one place
- **pi bond (π bond)**: the second bond in a double bond, formed by side-by-side p-orbital overlap; its electron pair can participate in resonance
- **curved arrow**: shows where an electron pair starts and where that electron pair goes

## Required learning flow

Each term starts with a **no-clue written explanation**. The learner sees the term only. No definition, word bank, matching cards, or multiple-choice options are visible. She must write what the term means in her own words and answer one short application question that shows she can use the idea.

The checker grades concept parts, not exact wording. Synonyms and plain-language explanations are accepted. A single familiar keyword, a clue fragment, or a list of rubric words cannot pass. The checker must also reject a response that contains required vocabulary while explicitly stating a conflicting idea.

Correct negative language must still be understood in context. Statements such as “atoms do not move in resonance,” “the molecule does not switch between contributors,” “the sigma bond does not move,” or “the tail is not a plus sign” are correct ideas and must not be rejected merely because they contain words such as `not` or `does not`.

If the first explanation misses, the app stays on the same term and:
- preserves the learner's written answer so she can revise her own thinking;
- identifies the concept parts she already expressed correctly;
- identifies any expressed idea that conflicts with the definition;
- identifies the concept parts that are still missing;
- gives a **word bank only after the miss**;
- includes useful words plus plausible distractors; and
- requires the learner to rewrite the explanation herself.

The word bank is support, not proof. The app never assembles the sentence for her.

If the learner chooses **I do not know yet**, the app may teach the meaning directly rather than forcing random guessing from a word bank. The meaning must then be hidden before the learner explains it again. That immediate recall remains supported practice and cannot by itself mark the term Independent.

## Games

Recognition games are practice only. Day 3 may use multiple choice or term-to-definition matching after the first production round to strengthen memory and make vocabulary practice more engaging. A game score never clears a term.

A term that needed a word bank, explicit teaching, or any other clue must return in a **later no-clue retrieval** after other activity has intervened. Only a correct later written explanation and application answer can move that term to **Independent**.

If the later no-clue retrieval still fails, the term becomes **Needs review** rather than being passed by recognition. The learner can stop and return to it instead of being forced through an automatic infinite loop.

## Migration rule for prior Day 3 progress

Progress saved under the earlier recognition-only vocabulary flow is not accepted as proof of vocabulary understanding. If a learner already advanced into **Teach, Guided, Practice, Transfer, Mastered, or Developing** before the production vocabulary requirement was introduced, the production vocabulary gate must take over before that Day 3 progress can continue. The prerequisite formal-charge gate is not reopened.

A previous vocabulary state can be considered complete only when the current production version records all five terms as **Independent** through no-clue writing and application.

Vocabulary evidence remains separate from resonance problem-solving evidence. Recognition does not prove vocabulary understanding, and solving a resonance problem does not silently erase a vocabulary gap.
