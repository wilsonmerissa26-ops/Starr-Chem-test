# PART B: CHEMISTRY

## B1. Valence Electrons

Valence electrons are outer-shell electrons available for bonding. H=1, C=4, N=5, O=6 for today's molecules. Carbon is Group 14, nitrogen 15, oxygen 16, fluorine 17. Hydrogen is full at 2 electrons; today's neutral H/C/N/O structures commonly follow the octet pattern.

## B2. Lewis Structure Method

1. Count total valence electrons.
2. Arrange atoms; hydrogen is never central.
3. Connect with single bonds.
4. Place lone pairs on outer atoms, then central atom.
5. Check that drawn electrons exactly match Step 1.

## B3. Worked Example: CH₄

C contributes 4 and four H contribute 4, total 8. Four C–H bonds use all 8. Carbon has an octet; each hydrogen has 2.

## B4. Guided Example: NH₃

N contributes 5 and three H contribute 3, total 8. Three N–H bonds use 6. The remaining 2 electrons form one lone pair on nitrogen.

## B5. Independent Production: H₂O

Correct structure: O central, two O–H single bonds, two lone pairs on O. Total 8; bonds use 4; remaining 4 make two lone pairs.

Hints progress from total count, to bonds used, to explicit remaining-electron placement. Repeated error switches to drawing all eight electrons as individual dots before grouping.

## B6. Independent Production: CH₃OH

Correct structure: carbon bonded to three H and O; O bonded to C and H with two lone pairs. Total 14; five bonds use 10; remaining 4 form two lone pairs on oxygen.

## B7. Chemistry cleared criteria

1. Independent correct H₂O.
2. Independent correct CH₃OH.
3. Correct-shaped explanation on at least one follow-up.
