# RS-I5 — Held-out five-membered-ring versus chair comparison

Use fresh unannotated 3D models of:
- **methylcyclopentane** in a puckered five-membered-ring conformation, and
- **chair cyclohexane**.

The methyl group is present only to keep the cold molecule distinct from the unsubstituted cyclopentane used in Guided. Do not use the methyl substituent itself as the deciding reason for the ring-strain ranking.

Prompt:

**Focus on the ring frameworks. Which ring has lower ring strain in this comparison, and what two geometric features support your answer?**

## Scoring contract

Required propositions:
1. chair cyclohexane is lower in ring strain in this introductory comparison,
2. chair cyclohexane keeps its C-C-C angles near tetrahedral,
3. chair cyclohexane keeps neighboring bonds largely staggered,
4. the five-membered ring in methylcyclopentane has relatively favorable bond angles but retains more torsional strain than chair cyclohexane,
5. the methyl substituent is not the deciding cause of the ring-strain ranking,
6. do not say cyclohexane is lower simply because six is a larger number than five.

Accept example:
> "Chair cyclohexane has lower ring strain because its ring angles are near tetrahedral and its neighboring bonds can be largely staggered. The five-membered ring in methylcyclopentane has fairly good angles but more residual torsional strain. The methyl group is not why I ranked the rings this way."

Wrong-but-keyword-complete example that must fail:
> "Methylcyclopentane is more strained only because the methyl group is bulky, while chair cyclohexane is lower because six-membered rings are always less strained than five-membered rings."

Why it fails: it ignores the ring-framework geometry, uses substituent bulk as the deciding cause, and replaces the taught angle/torsional reasoning with an invalid universal ring-size rule.

Second failure that must fail:
> "Methylcyclopentane has lower ring strain because its five-membered ring has near-tetrahedral angles, while chair cyclohexane is higher because its bonds are eclipsed."

Why it fails: it notices one favorable feature of the five-membered ring but reverses the bond-alignment relationship in chair cyclohexane and therefore reaches the wrong ranking.

## Independent-evidence rule

Objective ranking and explanation are scored separately.

A correct `chair cyclohexane` selection with an explanation based only on `six is bigger than five` does not create explanation evidence.

A response that initially gives correct angle/torsional reasoning and then contradicts it by claiming the methyl substituent is the sole cause does not pass explanation evidence.

If any support is requested or any answer-revealing geometry overlay is shown, this item becomes supported practice and cannot count as clean cold evidence.
