## Replacement for NAM-I1 required proposition 3

Parent cold item: **3-methylhexane**.

Replace the incorrect statement that locant 3 is obtained from "either equivalent numbering direction" with:

3. Numbering the six-carbon parent from one end places the methyl substituent at **C3**; numbering from the opposite end places it at **C4**. The correct numbering direction is the one that gives **3 rather than 4**, because the substituent must receive the lower locant.

The required final name remains:

`3-methylhexane`

### Role-preserving failure that must not pass

> "Either 3-methylhexane or 4-methylhexane is equally acceptable because reversing the chain gives an equivalent numbering direction."

Why it fails:
- the two numbering directions do **not** produce equivalent locants,
- one gives 3 and the other gives 4,
- the lower-locant rule selects 3.

Objective and explanation evidence remain separate. A learner who writes `3-methylhexane` but claims that 3 and 4 are equivalent does not earn clean explanation evidence.

## Replacement for NAM-I5 wrong-answer handling

Parent prompt remains:

**Draw/rebuild 2,2,4-trimethylpentane from the name, then identify the parent and branch carbons.**

Required structure evidence remains:
- five-carbon parent,
- three methyl substituents,
- connectivity corresponding to methyl groups at `2,2,4` under the preferred IUPAC numbering,
- total carbon count 8,
- normal carbon valence.

### Important numbering distinction

A physical drawing of this molecule does **not** become chemically wrong merely because the learner temporarily numbers the same pentane parent from the opposite end and therefore writes the locant sequence `2,4,4` on the page. That opposite numbering describes the **same connectivity**.

The naming rule is applied after both directions are compared:
- preferred direction: `2,2,4`,
- opposite direction: `2,4,4`,
- first point of difference: second locant, `2 < 4`,
- therefore the preferred IUPAC numbering is `2,2,4`.

### Wrong-but-keyword-complete explanation that must fail

> "My pentane structure has three methyl groups and I numbered them 2,4,4. That numbering is just as correct as 2,2,4 because reversing a parent chain always gives an equivalent locant set."

Why it fails:
- the **physical structure may be correct**,
- but the explanation incorrectly treats the two locant sets as equivalent,
- `2,2,4` wins over `2,4,4` at the first point of difference.

### Scoring rule

Score these separately:
1. **structure objective:** does the drawing have the correct connectivity and valence for 2,2,4-trimethylpentane?
2. **numbering explanation:** after comparing both directions, does the learner identify `2,2,4` as the preferred locant set and explain why?

A correct structure paired with the false claim that `2,4,4` is an equally preferred IUPAC numbering does not earn clean explanation evidence.
