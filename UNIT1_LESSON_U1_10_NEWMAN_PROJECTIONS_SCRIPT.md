# UNIT 1 LESSON U1-10 — NEWMAN PROJECTIONS FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES
## Fully authored beginner lesson script

**Status:** content draft for instructional review before runtime implementation.

**Parent curriculum:** `UNIT1_BEGINNER_TEACHING_CURRICULUM.md`

**Teaching-engine contract:** `DR_MERISSA_TEACHING_ENGINE_SPEC.md`

**Template standard:** `UNIT1_LESSON_U1_01_BOND_LINE_SCRIPT.md`

**Purpose of this file:** define exactly what the learner sees, hears, predicts, builds, explains, and is asked to do so Newman projections are taught as a 3D viewing skill rather than memorized circles and lines.

---

# 1. LESSON GOAL

A learner who begins with little or no usable knowledge of Newman projections should leave able to:

1. identify the exact C—C bond being viewed,
2. use a stated viewing direction to determine the front and back carbon,
3. identify which substituents belong to the front carbon and which belong to the back carbon,
4. understand why the front carbon is represented by a dot and the back carbon by a circle,
5. convert a fresh structure into a Newman projection without swapping front/back substituents,
6. distinguish staggered from eclipsed conformations,
7. distinguish anti from gauche when comparing the specified larger groups in a staggered Newman projection,
8. connect eclipsing and crowding to relative conformational energy at the qualitative level needed before the separate energy-diagram lesson,
9. explain why rotating around the C—C bond changes conformation while merely rotating the page does not,
10. solve a fresh Newman problem without prompts.

This lesson is not complete because the learner can recognize a dot and circle. It is complete when she can reconstruct what that dot and circle mean and build the projection from a new molecule and viewing direction.

# 2. LEARNER-FACING ORIENTATION

## Screen title

**Newman projections: put your eye on the carbon-carbon bond**

## Dr. Merissa narration

> "A Newman projection is not a random wheel. It is a view of a real molecule from one very specific direction. Imagine lining your eye up with a carbon-carbon single bond and looking straight down that bond. One carbon is closer to you. One is behind it. The Newman projection is simply a way to draw what you would see from that viewpoint."

## Immediate learner check

Visual: show a simple 3D-ish carbon chain with one C—C bond highlighted and a camera/eye icon off to one side.

Prompt:

**If the camera moves to the opposite end of the same highlighted C—C bond, could the carbon that was in front become the carbon in back?**

Choices:
- Yes
- No
- I am not sure yet

Correct: **Yes**

If correct:

> "Exactly. The molecule did not change, but the viewing direction did. That is why front versus back must come from the arrow or stated viewpoint, not from guessing."

If incorrect or unsure:

> "We are going to make that visible first. Front and back are not permanent names for the carbons. They depend on which direction you look down the bond."

This is orientation only, not mastery evidence.

# 3. SMALLEST PREREQUISITE GATE

Do not test nomenclature or energy diagrams here. Check only what Newman construction truly depends on.

## Gate item P1 — identify the target bond

Visual: show a four-carbon chain with the middle C2—C3 bond highlighted.

Prompt:

**Which two carbons make the highlighted bond we are going to look down?**

Correct: **C2 and C3**

## Gate item P2 — identify attached groups

Visual: keep C2 selected in a fully explicit or expanded representation of butane.

Prompt:

**How many groups are attached to this tetrahedral carbon besides the carbon on the other side of the viewing bond?**

Correct: **3**

If missed, show the three non-axis bonds one at a time and count them. The learner does not need the word `substituent` yet.

## Gate item P3 — single-bond rotation

Visual: simple model with one C—C single bond as the central axis.

Prompt:

**Can the groups on one carbon rotate around a carbon-carbon single bond without breaking that C—C bond?**

Correct: **Yes**

If missed:

Dr. Merissa:

> "A carbon-carbon single bond can rotate. The bond stays connected while the groups around it change their positions relative to one another. Those different arrangements are called conformations."

Low-risk interaction: drag one side of a simplified model through a small rotation while the central C—C bond remains intact.

Once prerequisite repair is correct, continue. Do not repeat the full gate.

# 4. TEACHING SEQUENCE — I DO

## Core worked molecule

Use **butane viewed down the C2—C3 bond from C2 toward C3**.

Keep carbon numbers available during instruction only.

The learner controls **Next**, **Back**, **Replay current step**, and **Pause**. Nothing auto-advances.

---

## WATCH STEP 1 — Identify the exact bond before drawing anything

### Visual

Show butane in a 3D-ish wedge/line or manipulable molecular model. Label carbons temporarily C1—C2—C3—C4. Highlight only C2—C3.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Before we draw a Newman projection, we have to answer one question: which bond are we looking down? Here it is the bond between carbon 2 and carbon 3. Everything we draw next is organized around this one bond."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**Tap the C2—C3 bond.**

A tap on a neighboring bond gets:

> "That bond would give a different Newman projection. Use the bond named in the viewing instruction."

---

## WATCH STEP 2 — Establish the viewing direction

### Visual

Place an eye/camera icon on the C2 side with an arrow pointing **C2 → C3** along the highlighted bond.

### Dr. Merissa

> "The arrow tells us the viewing direction. We are looking from carbon 2 toward carbon 3. Carbon 2 is closer to our eye, so carbon 2 is the **front carbon**. Carbon 3 is farther away, so carbon 3 is the **back carbon**."

Vocabulary is introduced only now:

> "Front carbon means the carbon closer to the viewer. Back carbon means the carbon farther from the viewer along the same bond."

### Prediction

Prompt:

**If the viewing arrow were reversed to C3 → C2, which carbon would become the front carbon?**

Correct: **C3**

If missed, physically move the eye icon to the other side and ask which carbon it reaches first.

---

## WATCH STEP 3 — List the three groups attached to each carbon

### Visual

Temporarily isolate C2 and C3 while keeping the central bond visible.

For butane viewed C2 → C3:
- front carbon C2 has `CH3`, `H`, `H` besides C3,
- back carbon C3 has `CH3`, `H`, `H` besides C2.

Use three attachment badges around each carbon.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Each carbon on the viewing bond has three other groups attached to it. Those groups are what become the six spokes in the Newman projection. Before we worry about angles, we make sure every group stays attached to the correct carbon."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**Drag the three group labels that belong to C2 into the FRONT tray.**

Correct set: `CH3`, `H`, `H`.

Then:

**Drag the three group labels that belong to C3 into the BACK tray.**

Correct set: `CH3`, `H`, `H`.

If a group is placed on the wrong carbon, do not continue to the circle. Keep the original molecule visible and trace that group's bond back to its carbon.

---

## WATCH STEP 4 — Move the camera into the bond axis

### Visual

Animate the entire molecule/camera relationship until the highlighted C2—C3 bond points directly toward the viewer. Do not change connectivity or rotate only the page.

The front carbon begins to visually overlap the back carbon.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Now we line our eye up exactly with the C2—C3 bond. From this direction, the front carbon blocks the center of the back carbon. That overlap is why Newman projections use a dot for the front carbon and a circle for the back carbon."

### Prediction

Prompt:

**Which carbon should be represented by the center dot: the one closer to you or the one farther away?**

Correct: **the one closer to you**

---

## WATCH STEP 5 — Transform the two carbons into dot and circle

### Visual

Carry out the transformation in place:

1. C2 shrinks into a **center dot** without moving its three bonds.
2. C3 expands into a **circle behind the dot** without moving its three bonds.
3. Keep a temporary label `front = C2` by the dot and `back = C3` by the circle.

### Dr. Merissa

> "The dot is the front carbon. Its three bonds start at the center. The circle is the back carbon. Its three bonds begin at the edge of the circle because that carbon is behind the front one."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**Tap one front-carbon bond, then one back-carbon bond.**

Front-bond taps must originate from the center dot. Back-bond taps must originate from the circle edge.

If the learner swaps them:

> "Follow where the bond begins. Center dot means front. Circle edge means back."

---

## WATCH STEP 6 — Carry substituents into the Newman view one at a time

### Visual

Return momentarily to the aligned 3D view and transfer each group into its Newman spoke while preserving front/back ownership.

Use an initial **staggered anti butane conformation** for the worked example:
- front `CH3` placed at 12 o'clock,
- front H groups at approximately 4 and 8 o'clock,
- back `CH3` placed at 6 o'clock,
- back H groups at approximately 2 and 10 o'clock.

Exact clock positions are implementation aids; the chemistry requirement is 120° separation around each carbon and 60° offset between front/back sets.

### Dr. Merissa

> "I am not choosing six random spokes. I am carrying each real bond from the molecule into the view. The three front bonds stay attached to the dot. The three back bonds stay attached to the circle."

### Prediction pause

Before moving the back `CH3`:

Prompt:

**Should this back-carbon CH3 bond begin at the center dot or at the back circle?**

Correct: **the back circle**

If missed, trace the `CH3` bond to C3 in the 3D model and return.

---

## WATCH STEP 7 — Name the staggered relationship only after seeing it

### Visual

Show the completed anti butane Newman. Highlight that each back bond lies between two front bonds rather than directly behind one.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Notice that the back bonds sit in the gaps between the front bonds. None of the front and back bonds line up directly. Chemists call this **staggered**."

Plain-language definition:

> "Staggered means the bonds on the back carbon are offset from the bonds on the front carbon."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**Do any front and back bonds lie directly on top of each other in this view?**

Correct: **No**

---

## WATCH STEP 8 — Introduce dihedral angle with a visible pair, not a definition dump

### Visual

Highlight the front `CH3` bond and back `CH3` bond. Draw an arc around the viewing axis from one highlighted bond direction to the other.

### Dr. Merissa

> "To describe how far apart two groups are around this bond, we measure the angle between their bond directions while looking down the C—C axis. That angle is called a **dihedral angle**."

In the shown anti conformation, display `180°` between the two `CH3` groups.

> "Here the two methyl groups are opposite each other, 180 degrees apart. In a staggered conformation, chemists call this relationship **anti**."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**In this Newman projection, are the two CH3 groups close together or as far apart as they can be around the bond?**

Correct: **as far apart as they can be**

---

## WATCH STEP 9 — Rotate around the C—C bond by 60°

### Visual

Lock the front carbon in place. Rotate only the **back carbon and its three attached groups** by 60° around the central C2—C3 axis.

The central bond remains connected.

New state: an **eclipsed** conformation.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Now we rotate around the carbon-carbon single bond. The carbons stay connected. The groups on the back carbon move together because they are attached to that carbon. After a 60-degree rotation, each back bond lines up behind a front bond."

### Prediction before name reveal

Prompt:

**Compared with the staggered view, do the front and back bonds now line up more directly?**

Correct: **Yes**

Then introduce the term:

> "When front and back bonds line up in the viewing direction, the conformation is **eclipsed**."

---

## WATCH STEP 10 — Explain why eclipsed is higher energy before giving a ranking rule

### Visual

On one aligned front/back bond pair, show the electron-density/bond regions occupying the same viewing direction. Use a simple crowding/repulsion cue rather than decorative sparks.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Eclipsed bonds are less comfortable because bonding electron regions are forced into closer alignment. That extra repulsion is called **torsional strain**. So when we compare conformations of the same molecule, eclipsed arrangements are generally higher in energy than staggered arrangements."

Plain-language first:

> "Torsional strain is the energy penalty that comes from eclipsing bonds during rotation."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**Which arrangement should usually be lower in energy for the same molecule: staggered or eclipsed?**

Correct: **staggered**

If wrong, replay only the side-by-side overlap comparison.

---

## WATCH STEP 11 — Rotate another 60° to a gauche staggered conformation

### Visual

Rotate the back carbon another 60°. Bonds are staggered again, but the two `CH3` groups are now 60° apart.

### Dr. Merissa

> "We are staggered again, so we removed the eclipsing. But now the two methyl groups are only 60 degrees apart instead of 180. A staggered arrangement with the two groups we are comparing 60 degrees apart is called **gauche**."

### Comparison visual

Show anti and gauche side by side with the same front/back labels.

### Dr. Merissa

> "Both anti and gauche are staggered. Anti is lower in energy for butane because its two methyl groups are farther apart. Gauche is still staggered, but the larger methyl groups are closer and experience more steric crowding."

Define only now:

> "Steric crowding means groups take up space and become less comfortable when they are brought close together."

### Interaction

Prompt:

**Which butane conformation keeps the two CH3 groups farther apart: anti or gauche?**

Correct: **anti**

---

## WATCH STEP 12 — The page-rotation trap

### Visual

Start from one fixed Newman projection. Rotate the entire drawing/page 120° as a rigid object without changing any relative front/back bond positions.

Then reset and rotate only the back carbon 60° relative to the front carbon.

### Dr. Merissa

> "These are not the same operation. Turning the whole page changes how the drawing sits on your screen, but it does not change the conformation. Rotating around the C—C bond changes the relative positions of front and back groups, so that does change the conformation."

### Interaction

Show two animations labeled A and B.

Prompt:

**Which animation actually changes the molecular conformation?**

Correct: **the one that rotates one carbon relative to the other around the C—C bond**

# 5. QUICK CONCEPT CHECK BEFORE BUILD TOGETHER

This remains supported instruction, not independent evidence.

1. **The center dot represents the front carbon.** True.
2. **The circle represents a carbon behind the front carbon.** True.
3. **Reversing the viewing direction can swap which carbon is front and back.** True.
4. **Rotating the entire Newman drawing on the page automatically creates a new conformation.** False.
5. **Staggered means back bonds are offset from front bonds.** True.
6. **Anti and gauche are both staggered relationships.** True, when used for the compared groups in the context taught here.

If any response is wrong, revisit only the relevant visual transformation.

# 6. BUILD TOGETHER — WE DO

## Fresh molecule

Use **pentane viewed down C2—C3 from C2 toward C3**.

Do not reuse butane.

Display a structure where the learner can identify:
- front carbon C2 groups besides C3: `CH3`, `H`, `H`,
- back carbon C3 groups besides C2: `CH2CH3`, `H`, `H`.

Start from a blank Newman workspace plus the original molecule.

Dr. Merissa:

> "Now we will build one from scratch. I will ask the decisions in the order an organic chemist should make them."

## Build step 1 — viewing bond

Prompt:

**Tap the bond we are viewing down.**

Correct: C2—C3.

## Build step 2 — front/back from direction

Show arrow C2 → C3.

Prompt:

**Which carbon is front?** C2.

**Which carbon is back?** C3.

If either is wrong, move the eye icon along the axis before continuing.

## Build step 3 — group inventory

Learner drags each group into FRONT or BACK tray.

Correct:
- FRONT: `CH3`, `H`, `H`
- BACK: `CH2CH3`, `H`, `H`

## Build step 4 — place dot/circle framework

Learner taps:
- center for front carbon dot,
- outer circle for back carbon.

## Build step 5 — place front groups

System supplies three front spoke positions 120° apart. Learner assigns the three front groups.

Any overall rigid rotation of the entire set is acceptable if relative connectivity is preserved.

## Build step 6 — construct an anti staggered relationship between the largest carbon groups

Prompt:

**Place the back `CH2CH3` group so it is staggered and as far from the front `CH3` group as possible.**

Correct: 180° relationship between those two compared groups.

Back H groups fill remaining back positions.

## Build Together completion statement

> "You built the Newman by deciding the bond, the viewing direction, which groups belong to each carbon, and only then the angles. That order matters. A pretty circle with groups on it is not enough if the groups are attached to the wrong carbon."

Log as supported `NEWMAN_BUILD_TOGETHER_SUCCESS`, never independent evidence.

# 7. GUIDED PRACTICE — YOU DO WITH SUPPORT

## Fresh molecule

Use **2-methylbutane viewed down C2—C3 from C3 toward C2**.

The reversed viewing direction is deliberate so the learner cannot memorize "left carbon = front."

Structure relationships:
- viewing direction C3 → C2 makes C3 the front carbon,
- front C3 groups besides C2: `CH3`, `H`, `H`,
- back C2 groups besides C3: `CH3`, `CH3`, `H`.

Dr. Merissa:

> "This one is harder because the viewing arrow points the opposite way and the back carbon has two methyl groups. Use the same decision order."

## Guided task A — front/back

Prompt:

**From C3 → C2, which carbon is front?**

Correct: C3.

If wrong, show the eye icon on the C3 side and ask which carbon the eye reaches first.

## Guided task B — attach groups to the correct carbon

Learner sorts all six groups into FRONT and BACK trays.

If wrong, use a **bond-trace overlay**:
1. freeze the molecule,
2. highlight the chosen group,
3. trace its bond directly back to C2 or C3,
4. preserve the same group label as it moves into the matching tray.

Do not simply say "that group belongs to the other carbon."

## Guided task C — build the Newman framework

Learner places front spokes from the dot and back spokes from the circle.

System rejects front substituents placed on back spokes and vice versa.

## Guided task D — classify a shown conformation

Present a staggered state.

Prompt:

**Staggered or eclipsed?**

Then rotate to an eclipsed state.

Prompt:

**What changed that makes this eclipsed?**

Correct-shaped idea: front and back bond directions now align rather than sit in the gaps.

## Guided success criterion

Learner must make two consecutive guided decisions without a corrective reveal before support fades.

**What support fades means here:**
1. remove carbon-number badges first,
2. remove FRONT/BACK tray labels after the learner correctly identifies front/back twice,
3. replace step prompts with the single goal `Build the Newman projection from the stated viewing direction`,
4. keep `Show viewing arrow` and `Trace one bond` available only by learner request,
5. the entire current Guided item remains supported even after these scaffolds fade,
6. an error restores only the minimum scaffold for the failed subskill and resets the two-success guided streak.

Do not move to cold evidence until the Guided example is complete without unresolved front/back or attachment confusion.

# 8. MISCONCEPTION CONTRASTS

Every repair must show the relationship visually or interactively rather than repeat a rule.

## M1 — "The left carbon is always the front carbon"

### Wrong idea

Learner uses page position rather than viewing direction.

### Representation switch

Show the same molecule twice:
- view A: C2 → C3,
- view B: C3 → C2.

Move the eye icon from one end to the other while the molecule stays unchanged.

Dr. Merissa:

> "Front means closer to the viewer. Change the viewer, and front/back can switch even though the molecule is the same."

Repair: learner taps the front carbon for both directions.

---

## M2 — "Dot and circle are just decoration"

### Wrong idea

Learner places groups without preserving which carbon owns them.

### Representation switch

Color-independent labels:
- `FRONT` tag anchored to center dot,
- `BACK` tag anchored to circle,
- each original bond traced from molecule to its spoke.

Dr. Merissa:

> "The dot and circle encode two different carbons. A group attached to the front carbon cannot jump to a back-carbon spoke without changing the molecule."

Repair: drag one group from each carbon to the correct spoke set.

---

## M3 — "Rotating the page changes anti to gauche"

### Wrong idea

Learner treats rigid drawing rotation as bond rotation.

### Representation switch

Side-by-side:
- left: whole Newman rotates as one rigid object,
- right: back carbon rotates relative to fixed front carbon.

Overlay a small `relative angle unchanged` label on the rigid rotation and `relative angle changed` on bond rotation.

Repair: identify which motion changes the CH3—CH3 dihedral relationship.

---

## M4 — "Staggered means anti"

### Wrong idea

Learner treats all staggered conformations as anti.

### Representation switch

Show butane anti and gauche side by side.

Both receive a `STAGGERED` badge. Only the 180° CH3/CH3 arrangement receives `ANTI`; the 60° arrangement receives `GAUCHE`.

Dr. Merissa:

> "Staggered describes whether front and back bonds are offset. Anti and gauche describe the relationship between the groups we are comparing inside a staggered conformation."

Repair: classify two fresh staggered views as anti or gauche relative to the highlighted groups.

---

## M5 — "Anti/gauche can be assigned just because two groups are 180°/60° on any picture"

### Wrong idea

Learner ignores whether the conformation is staggered and/or ignores the specified pair of groups.

### Representation switch

Highlight the exact compared groups first, then show their angle. Present one eclipsed example with a 0° relationship and one staggered example.

Dr. Merissa:

> "First identify the two groups being compared and whether the projection is staggered. Then use the dihedral relationship. Do not label the whole drawing anti or gauche from a random pair."

Repair: learner taps the two groups named in the prompt before choosing anti/gauche.

---

## M6 — "Eclipsed is higher because the drawing looks messy"

### Wrong idea

Learner has a visual preference but no causal model.

### Representation switch

Show aligned bond regions in eclipsed versus offset bond regions in staggered.

Dr. Merissa:

> "The energy difference is not because one drawing looks cleaner. Eclipsing brings bonding electron regions into less favorable alignment, creating torsional strain."

Repair: choose which state has more bond alignment and connect that to higher torsional strain.

# 9. SIX-WAY "I DON'T KNOW" CONTENT ROUTING

The shared router remains authoritative.

Response:

## IDK 3 — "I forgot something I need"

Offer targeted micro-reviews:
- "I forgot how to tell which groups are attached to each carbon."
- "I forgot what front and back mean."
- "I forgot what staggered/eclipsed means."
- "I forgot what anti/gauche means."

Repair only the selected prerequisite, then use a fresh item.

## IDK 4 — "I started but got stuck"

Preserve learner work and ask:

**Where did you get stuck?**
- choosing the viewing bond,
- deciding front/back,
- sorting substituents,
- placing front/back spokes,
- staggered vs eclipsed,
- anti vs gauche.

Repair only that step.

## IDK 5 — "I need to see an example"

Show a new worked example not used in cold evidence. Use full Watch controls and a different molecule/viewing direction from butane.

Then return to a fresh item.

## IDK 6 — "This explanation isn't making sense"

Switch representation to **Camera-On-Bond Mode**:
1. show the molecule as a manipulable 3D-ish model,
2. place a camera icon physically beside the first carbon in the viewing direction,
3. animate the camera traveling until the C—C bond is centered,
4. keep the front carbon opaque while the back carbon becomes partly transparent,
5. freeze and label every attached group before any Newman symbols appear,
6. morph front carbon to dot and back carbon to circle without moving attached bond directions.

If this still fails, use a **two-disc physical model analogy**:

> "Imagine two three-spoke wheels mounted on the same axle, one in front of the other. The axle is the C—C bond. Looking straight down the axle makes the front hub appear in front of the back hub. Turning only one wheel changes their relative arrangement; turning the entire pair together does not."

Immediately map the analogy back to the molecule and Newman projection.

# 10. INDEPENDENT PRACTICE — COLD EVIDENCE BANK

These items must never appear during Teach, Watch, Build Together, Guided, or IDK worked examples.

During cold evidence:
- no front/back tray,
- no carbon-number overlay unless numbering is part of the problem statement,
- no camera animation,
- no bond tracing,
- no hints,
- no automatic angle labels,
- no conformational classification badge.

If help is requested, the current item becomes supported practice and cannot count as independent evidence.

## NP-I1 — Front/back from viewing direction

Fresh molecule with a specified bond and direction, e.g. `look from C3 toward C2`.

Prompt:

**Which carbon is front and which is back?**

Both must be correct.

## NP-I2 — Substituent ownership

Fresh structure with asymmetric front/back groups.

Prompt:

**List the three groups attached to the front carbon and the three attached to the back carbon, excluding the C—C viewing bond itself.**

The grader must preserve group-to-carbon ownership.

## NP-I3 — Build a fresh Newman projection

Provide a fresh molecule and viewing direction.

Prompt:

**Draw the Newman projection exactly as the molecule is shown.**

System checks:
- correct front/back assignment,
- correct three substituents on each carbon,
- correct relative arrangement,
- no front/back swap.

A rigid rotation of the entire completed Newman on the page is equivalent if all relative relationships are preserved.

## NP-I4 — Staggered/eclipsed classification

Show two fresh Newman projections.

Prompt:

**Classify each as staggered or eclipsed and tell me what visual relationship you used.**

Correct-shaped explanation must distinguish offset versus aligned front/back bonds.

## NP-I5 — Anti/gauche classification

Show a fresh staggered Newman with two highlighted groups.

Prompt:

**Are the highlighted groups anti or gauche? Explain using their dihedral relationship.**

Correct:
- anti if 180° apart,
- gauche if 60° apart,
for the specified staggered comparisons used in this lesson.

## NP-I6 — Relative stability of butane conformers

Show fresh anti, gauche, and eclipsed butane views in a shuffled order.

Prompt:

**Rank them from lowest to highest energy and explain the main reason for your ranking.**

Minimum correct causal structure:
- anti staggered lowest among shown because methyl groups are farthest apart,
- gauche staggered higher than anti due greater steric crowding between methyl groups,
- eclipsed higher than staggered due torsional strain, with greater group-group eclipsing/crowding raising energy further where applicable.

Do not require a numerical energy difference in this lesson.

## NP-I7 — Page rotation versus bond rotation

Show two before/after pairs.

Prompt:

**Which pair represents a new conformation and which is only the same Newman drawing rotated on the page? Explain.**

# 12. EXPLAIN-WHY PROMPTS — ROLE-PRESERVING SCORING CONTRACT

As established by the approved U1-01 template, explanation grading must preserve **relationships and roles**, not keywords.

A keyword-only, unordered-token, or "contains all expected words" grader is not allowed.

## E-W1 — Why does reversing the viewing direction swap front/back?

Prompt:

**Why can the same C—C bond give the opposite front/back assignment when the viewing direction is reversed?**

Required propositions:
1. front/back are defined relative to the viewer,
2. the carbon reached first from the stated viewing direction is the front carbon,
3. reversing the viewing direction reverses which carbon is reached first,
4. the molecule/connectivity itself did not need to change.

Acceptable:
- "Front is whichever carbon is closer to your eye. If I look from the other end, I reach the other carbon first, so front and back switch even though it is the same bond."

Wrong-but-keyword-complete response that must fail:
- "Reversing the viewing direction changes which carbon is actually bonded, so the back carbon becomes chemically attached in front."

Why it fails: it incorrectly changes connectivity instead of only viewpoint.

## E-W2 — Why is the dot front and circle back?

Prompt:

**Why do the front-carbon bonds begin at the center dot while the back-carbon bonds begin at the circle?**

Required propositions:
1. the viewer is looking straight down the C—C axis,
2. the front carbon lies closer and visually blocks the center of the back carbon,
3. the center dot encodes the front carbon,
4. the circle encodes the back carbon behind it,
5. attached groups must remain on the spokes belonging to their actual carbon.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete response that must fail:
- "The circle is the front carbon surrounding the dot, and the dot is the back carbon whose bonds start at the outer edge."

Why it fails: front/back roles and spoke origins are reversed.

## E-W3 — Why is staggered generally lower than eclipsed?

Prompt:

**Why is a staggered conformation generally lower in energy than an eclipsed conformation of the same molecule?**

Required propositions:
1. eclipsed front/back bond directions align,
2. this creates less favorable bond-electron alignment / torsional strain,
3. staggered bonds are offset and reduce that eclipsing interaction.

Acceptable:
- "In eclipsed, the front and back bonds line up, which increases torsional strain. Staggered offsets the bonds, so that interaction is lower."

Wrong-but-keyword-complete response that must fail:
- "Staggered has more torsional strain because the bonds are offset, while eclipsed is lower because the bonds line up."

Why it fails: it reverses the energy relationship and cause.

## E-W4 — Why is anti lower than gauche for butane?

Prompt:

**Why is the anti staggered conformation of butane lower in energy than the gauche staggered conformation?**

Required propositions:
1. both are staggered,
2. the compared methyl groups are 180° apart in anti and 60° apart in gauche,
3. anti keeps the methyl groups farther apart,
4. gauche has greater steric crowding between the methyl groups.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete response that must fail:
- "Gauche is lower because its methyl groups are 60° apart and farther apart than the 180° anti groups."

Why it fails: it reverses spatial relationship and stability.

## E-W5 — Why does rotating the page not create a new conformation?

Prompt:

**Why does turning the entire Newman drawing on the page not create a new molecular conformation?**

Required propositions:
1. a rigid page rotation moves front and back groups together,
2. relative front/back angular relationships stay the same,
3. a conformational change requires rotation about the C—C bond so one side changes relative to the other.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete response that must fail:
- "Rotating the page changes the C—C dihedral angles because every spoke changes screen position, so it creates a new conformation."

Why it fails: screen orientation changed, not relative molecular geometry.

If a response includes a correct proposition and a contradiction, do not pass it. Use a fresh explanation prompt after repair.

# 13. TRANSFER TASKS

## Transfer NP-T1 — reverse-view reconstruction

Show one completed Newman projection for a molecule viewed A → B.

Prompt:

**Now imagine viewing the same bond from B → A. Without changing connectivity, construct the corresponding Newman projection from the opposite viewing direction.**

Required:
- front/back ownership swaps appropriately,
- same groups remain attached to same real carbons,
- relative 3D conformation remains chemically equivalent.

This checks viewpoint understanding rather than memorized spoke positions.

## Transfer NP-T2 — explain a classmate's front/back error

Prompt:

**A classmate built the right six groups but placed every group from the front carbon on the circle and every group from the back carbon on the dot. Explain exactly what is wrong.**

Required explanation:
- center dot = front carbon,
- circle = back carbon,
- groups cannot switch carbon ownership merely because all six labels are present.

## Transfer NP-T3 — same conformation or new conformation?

Show three drawings:
1. original Newman,
2. whole drawing rigidly rotated,
3. back carbon rotated relative to front.

Prompt:

**Which drawings represent the same conformation and which represent a different conformation? Explain the deciding relationship.**

# 14. LATER RETRIEVAL

After at least one different lesson or meaningful activity intervenes, present a fresh asymmetric molecule and a new viewing direction.

Ask only:
1. identify front/back,
2. construct or complete the Newman projection,
3. classify it as staggered/eclipsed or anti/gauche if appropriate.

Do not reopen the full beginner lesson if retrieval is clean.

If explanation evidence already exists, a clean later fresh construction can satisfy the second-cold-item mastery component.

# 15. ADAPTIVE TEST-OUT PATH

Before full teaching, give a 3-item cold probe:

1. identify front/back from a specified viewing direction,
2. correctly assign substituents to front versus back,
3. classify/build one simple Newman projection.

### 3/3 clean

Skip full Teach/Watch. Give later light confirmation plus explanation if not already established.

### 2/3

Teach only the failed subskill and verify with a fresh item.

### 0–1/3

Run the full lesson.

Any hint, camera overlay, group trace, or front/back label contaminates that probe item.

# 16. ACCESSIBILITY + PHONE/IPAD REQUIREMENTS

1. Front/back cannot be communicated by color alone; use `FRONT`, `BACK`, dot/circle geometry, labels, and/or patterns.
2. Every camera/molecule transformation needs an equivalent text description.
3. Reduced-motion mode must preserve the instructional sequence with discrete states: side view → axis-aligned view → dot/circle mapping → completed Newman.
4. The learner must be able to replay the camera alignment and dot/circle morph without replaying the whole lesson.
5. Drag/tap targets for spokes and substituent labels must be large enough for touch input.
6. A group dropped on the wrong carbon's spoke must receive specific ownership feedback rather than silently snapping somewhere else.
7. Portrait mode must keep the original molecule and Newman workspace visible enough to compare, using a vertical stack if needed.
8. Landscape may use side-by-side molecule/Newman views.
9. Voice narration is optional; all reasoning remains available in text.
10. Any 3D-like visual must include a non-motion alternative for learners who cannot use rotation gestures.
11. The return destination to Test 1 support must be preserved.

# 20. DEFINITION OF DONE FOR U1-10

The lesson is successful when a beginner can take a new molecule and viewing instruction and say, in her own reasoning:

- which C—C bond is being viewed,
- which carbon is front and which is back,
- which three groups belong to each carbon,
- why the front carbon becomes a dot and the back carbon a circle,
- how to place all six groups without swapping ownership,
- whether the resulting conformation is staggered or eclipsed,
- whether specified groups are anti or gauche when appropriate,
- why eclipsing raises energy and why anti butane is lower than gauche butane,
- why rotating the whole page is not the same as rotating around the C—C bond,

and can then build a fresh Newman projection without the teaching screen.

The standard is not:

> "She can recognize a Newman projection."

The standard is:

> **"She can mentally put her eye on the bond, preserve which groups belong to which carbon, and reconstruct the projection from the molecule itself."**
