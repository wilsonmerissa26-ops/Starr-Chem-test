# DAY 7 CURRICULUM — NUCLEOPHILE, ELECTROPHILE, POLARITY, AND ELECTRON FLOW

## 1. Purpose and scope
electron-pair donors/acceptors, polarized bonds, and two-arrow substitution readiness. This is readiness preparation, not a replacement for Organic Chemistry I.

### Chemistry accuracy anchors
- A nucleophile donates an electron pair; an electrophile accepts it.
- Neutral CH3Br has an electrophilic carbon because the C-Br bond is polarized.
- Arrow tails begin at electrons; in HO- plus CH3Br, one arrow forms C-O and the C-Br pair goes to Br.

## 14. Auditable runtime question and answer-key bank

The wording below is the learner-visible production task. Fallbacks are fresh equivalents and never repair the contaminated original.

### D7-G-ROLES — guided

**Question:** In HO− + CH3Br, identify the nucleophile and electrophilic atom.

**Chemistry answer key:** HO− is the electron-pair-donating nucleophile; polarized CH3Br has an electrophilic carbon.

**Semantic evidence required:** `ho-` or `hydroxide`; `nucleophile`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `polar`.

### D7-I-ROLES — independent

**Question:** In NH3 + CH3Cl, identify the nucleophile and electrophilic atom and justify each role.

**Chemistry answer key:** NH3 donates its lone pair; the polarized C–Cl bond makes carbon electron-poor and electrophilic.

**Semantic evidence required:** `nh3` or `ammonia`; `nucleophile`; `lone pair`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `polar`.

### D7-F-ROLES — independent fallback

**Question:** Fresh equivalent: identify roles in CN− + CH3I.

**Chemistry answer key:** CN− is nucleophile; methyl carbon is electrophilic because C–I is polarized.

**Semantic evidence required:** `cn-` or `cyanide`; `nucleophile`; `electron pair` or `negative`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `polar`.

### D7-I-SOURCE — independent

**Question:** For HO− attacking CH3Br, state the electron source and destination of the bond-forming arrow.

**Chemistry answer key:** The arrow tail starts at an oxygen lone pair and points to carbon to form C–O.

**Semantic evidence required:** `lone pair` or `electron pair`; `oxygen`; `carbon`; `c-o` or `bond`.

### D7-F-SOURCE — independent fallback

**Question:** Fresh equivalent: describe the bond-forming arrow for NH3 attacking CH3I.

**Chemistry answer key:** The tail starts at nitrogen’s lone pair and the head points to carbon, forming C–N.

**Semantic evidence required:** `lone pair`; `nitrogen`; `carbon`; `c-n` or `bond`.

### D7-I-ARROWS — independent

**Question:** Describe both curved arrows for HO− + CH3Br and explain why the second is required.

**Chemistry answer key:** O lone pair → carbon forms C–O; C–Br pair → Br breaks C–Br and prevents five bonds at carbon.

**Semantic evidence required:** `oxygen` or `lone pair`; `carbon`; `c-o` or `form`; `c-br` or `bond`; `bromine` or `br-`; `valence` or `four bond` or `octet`.

### D7-F-ARROWS — independent fallback

**Question:** Fresh equivalent: describe both arrows for NH3 + CH3I.

**Chemistry answer key:** N lone pair → carbon and C–I pair → iodine.

**Semantic evidence required:** `nitrogen` or `lone pair`; `carbon`; `c-n` or `form`; `c-i` or `bond`; `iodine` or `i-`; `valence` or `octet`.

### D7-T-CYANIDE — transfer

**Question:** Describe roles and both arrows when CN− reacts with CH3Cl.

**Chemistry answer key:** CN− donates an electron pair to methyl carbon to form C–C while the C–Cl pair moves to Cl−.

**Semantic evidence required:** `cn-` or `cyanide`; `nucleophile`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `electron pair`; `c-c` or `bond`; `c-cl` or `bond`; `chlorine` or `cl-`.

### D7-T-CYANIDE-FALLBACK — transfer fallback

**Question:** Fresh transfer: describe roles and arrows for HS− + CH3I.

**Chemistry answer key:** A sulfur lone pair forms C–S while C–I electrons go to iodine.

**Semantic evidence required:** `hs-` or `hydrosulfide`; `nucleophile`; `carbon`; `electrophil`; `sulfur` or `lone pair`; `c-s` or `bond`; `c-i` or `bond`; `iodine` or `i-`.

## 15. Support, retrieval, and routing behavior

The cold prompt shows no definition, choices, word bank, or support visual. Hint identifies the feature to inspect; First step asks for the species/result before its reason; Walkthrough and IDK reveal the complete keyed reasoning and contaminate that encounter. Wrong answers remain on the same problem for bounded repair. A correct contaminated response routes to the named fresh equivalent. Only a first-attempt, unsupported response to an Independent item counts. After supported teach-back, the later fallback is a fresh no-clue retrieval with a reset attempt budget. Notebook records clean versus supported encounters; Review records the misconception code; Summary reports clean evidence and transfer. Missing prerequisite evidence routes to that day’s Review without resetting completed days.
