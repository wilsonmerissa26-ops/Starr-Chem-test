# UNIT 1 LESSON U1-08 - SAME MOLECULE, CONSTITUTIONAL ISOMER, STEREOISOMER, OR NONE
## Fully authored beginner lesson script

**Status:** content draft for instructional review before runtime implementation.

**Purpose:** teach relationship classification by molecular formula, atom connectivity, and chemically meaningful spatial arrangement rather than by whether two drawings look different.

Cold items carry role-preserving scoring contracts from the start.

---

# 1. LESSON GOAL

A beginner should leave able to:

1. count/compare molecular formulas,
2. map which atoms are connected to which,
3. recognize the same molecule after rotation/redraw or ordinary conformational change,
4. define constitutional isomers as same molecular formula + different connectivity,
5. define stereoisomers at the current introductory level as same molecular formula + same connectivity + different non-conformational spatial arrangement,
6. distinguish a stereoisomeric change from ordinary single-bond rotation/conformation,
7. recognize `none` when formulas differ or the requested relationship criteria are not satisfied,
8. use cis/trans information as stereochemical evidence when explicitly given,
9. avoid `looks different = isomer`, `same formula = constitutional isomer`, and `same connectivity = same drawing only` shortcuts,
10. explain the decisive evidence for a fresh pair.

The key question sequence is:

> **Same formula? Same connectivity? If connectivity is the same, is the difference only a redraw/conformation, or is there a real stereochemical difference?**

---

# 2. SCOPE BOUNDARY

This lesson teaches the classification categories required by the Unit 1/historical Test 1 signal:
- same molecule,
- constitutional isomers,
- stereoisomers,
- none.

It does not attempt a complete stereochemistry course.

Do not introduce:
- R/S assignment,
- optical rotation,
- enantiomer/diastereomer taxonomy unless verified current course material requires it,
- E/Z priority rules beyond simple cis/trans examples used to establish the category.

Conformers produced by ordinary single-bond rotation are classified as **the same molecule** for this lesson, not as separate stereoisomers.

A chair flip that preserves connectivity and up/down stereochemistry is likewise the same molecule, though U1-13 teaches the chair mechanics separately.

---

# 3. ORIENTATION

Screen title:
**Different drawing does not automatically mean different molecule**

Dr. Merissa:

> "Chemists can draw the same molecule from different angles, in different conformations, or with a different page layout. Before calling two drawings isomers, we have to ask what actually changed: the formula, the connectivity, or a real stereochemical arrangement."

Immediate interaction:
Show one simple alkane rotated on the page without changing bonds.

**Did rotating the drawing break or reconnect any atoms?**

Correct: **No**.

---

# 4. SMALLEST PREREQUISITE GATE

## P1 - formula count

Give one bond-line structure and ask total C/H count.

If missed, U1-01 micro-repair.

## P2 - connectivity

Show carbon A bonded to B and C. Ask whether moving the picture without changing those bonds changes connectivity.

Correct: No.

## P3 - ordinary single-bond rotation

Show two conformations of the same C-C single bond.

Prompt:
**If only that single bond rotates and no connectivity changes, is this automatically a new constitutional molecule?**

Correct: No.

---

# 5. BUILD THE DECISION TREE BEFORE THE LABELS

## STEP A - Formula

Dr. Merissa:
> "First compare atom inventory. Isomers must have the same molecular formula. If the formulas differ, stop: they are not isomers of each other."

## STEP B - Connectivity

> "If formulas match, ask whether each atom is connected to the same kinds of neighboring atoms in the same pattern. Different connectivity gives constitutional isomers."

## STEP C - Spatial arrangement

> "If formula and connectivity both match, the pair may simply be the same molecule redrawn or in a different conformation. Only a genuine stereochemical difference that is not removed by ordinary conformational motion earns the stereoisomer category in this lesson."

---

# 6. TEACHING SEQUENCE - I DO

## WATCH 1 - Same molecule, redrawn

Use **2-methylbutane** in two bond-line orientations.

Atom-map carbons 1-5 in both drawings.

Show every carbon has the same neighbors.

Classification: **same molecule**.

Prediction:
**The page shape changed. Did any carbon get a new neighbor?**

Correct: No.

---

## WATCH 2 - Constitutional isomers

Compare:
- **n-butane** `CH3CH2CH2CH3`,
- **2-methylpropane** `(CH3)3CH`.

Both formula: C4H10.

Connectivity:
- n-butane has an unbranched four-carbon path,
- 2-methylpropane has a central carbon bonded to three other carbons.

Classification: **constitutional isomers**.

Dr. Merissa:
> "Same formula gets us through the first gate. Different connectivity decides constitutional isomer."

---

## WATCH 3 - Same formula alone is not enough to stop reasoning

Compare:
- ethanol `CH3CH2OH`,
- dimethyl ether `CH3OCH3`.

Both C2H6O.

Map O connectivity:
- ethanol O bonded to C and H,
- dimethyl ether O bonded to C and C.

Therefore constitutional isomers.

This explicitly links to U1-03 local-pattern reasoning.

---

## WATCH 4 - Conformers are still the same molecule

Use two Newman conformations of butane, one anti and one gauche.

Do not reteach Newman construction.

Show:
- same formula,
- same connectivity,
- one C-C single-bond rotation interconverts them.

Classification: **same molecule (different conformations)**.

Dr. Merissa:
> "A conformation can change energy and shape without changing molecular identity. In this classification task, conformers belong under same molecule."

---

## WATCH 5 - Stereoisomers

Use **cis-2-butene** and **trans-2-butene** as an introductory fixed-geometry contrast.

Show:
- same formula C4H8,
- same connectivity C-C=C-C,
- different relative placement of the two CH3 groups across the double bond,
- ordinary rotation around the C=C is not treated like free C-C single-bond rotation; changing cis to trans requires changing the stereochemical arrangement rather than simply redrawing/rotating the page.

Classification: **stereoisomers**.

Do not teach E/Z priority rules here.

---

## WATCH 6 - None

Compare:
- butane C4H10,
- pentane C5H12.

Formulas differ.

Classification: **none** for the requested isomer relationship.

Dr. Merissa:
> "Do not spend time comparing connectivity once the atom inventory already proves they cannot be isomers."

---

## WATCH 7 - Same connectivity does not mean every spatial drawing is stereoisomeric

Show the same 1,2-dichloroethane before/after rotation around its C-C single bond.

Classification: same molecule/conformers.

Contrast with cis/trans-2-butene.

Prompt:
**Which difference can be produced by ordinary single-bond rotation?**

Correct: the dichloroethane conformational difference.

---

# 7. BUILD TOGETHER - WE DO

Use a fresh pair:
- **pentane**,
- **2-methylbutane**.

Start with no labels.

Learner performs:
1. count formula of each -> both C5H12,
2. map carbon connectivity,
3. identify unbranched versus branched neighbor pattern,
4. choose constitutional isomers,
5. explain why `same formula` alone was not the final answer.

Supported only.

# 8. GUIDED PRACTICE

## Guided A - same redraw

Two rotated/redrawn forms of **3-methylhexane** with atom mapping available on request.

Correct: same molecule.

## Guided B - constitutional

**1-propanol** vs **2-propanol**, both C3H8O, different O-bearing carbon connectivity.

Correct: constitutional isomers.

## Guided C - conformer vs stereoisomer contrast

Pair 1: two ordinary single-bond conformations of a substituted ethane -> same molecule.

Pair 2: simple cis/trans alkene pair not used cold -> stereoisomers.

## Guided D - none

Two molecules with clearly different formulas.

### Scaffold fading

After two clean guided classifications:
1. hide formula totals,
2. hide atom mapping,
3. remove `same connectivity?` prompt,
4. reduce to `formula -> connectivity -> spatial meaning -> classification`,
5. hints learner-requested only.

# 9. MISCONCEPTIONS

## M1 - different drawing = different molecule

Representation switch: animate continuous page rotation with bonds preserved.

## M2 - same formula = same molecule

Use n-butane vs isobutane; same inventory, different connectivity.

## M3 - same formula = constitutional isomer automatically

Use cis/trans-2-butene; same formula but same connectivity, so not constitutional.

## M4 - same connectivity always means same molecule

Use cis/trans fixed stereochemical pair to show stereoisomer category.

## M5 - every 3D difference = stereoisomer

Use anti/gauche conformers to show ordinary bond rotation.

## M6 - page mirror/rotation changes stereochemistry automatically

Show a simple non-stereochemical molecule mirrored/redrawn with atom mapping preserved.

## M7 - chair flip creates a new stereoisomer

If chair prerequisite is available, show one chair flip preserving substituent up/down; classify as same molecule/conformation. Otherwise keep this repair deferred to U1-13.

## M8 - `none` means structures have nothing in common

Clarify `none` means they do not satisfy the listed relationship categories for the pair; they can still share features.

# 10. SIX IDK ROUTES

1. **Question meaning:** identify whether the task asks formula, connectivity, spatial arrangement, or final category.
2. **Don't know how to start:** compare formulas only.
3. **Forgot prerequisite:** atom counting or bond-line connectivity micro-review.
4. **Started/stuck:** preserve formula/connectivity work; repair only next decision.
5. **Need example:** use support-only pair not cold-reserved.
6. **Explanation unclear:** switch to **Atom-Mapping Mode**. Assign matching atom IDs, then show whether neighbor lists change. For stereochemistry, preserve IDs and highlight only the fixed relative spatial relation.

Any support contaminates current cold item.

# 11. COLD EVIDENCE BANK

No atom mapping, formula reveal, rotation animation, connectivity badges, or answer hints.

Cold-reserved:
- two redraws of 2-methylpentane for ISO-I1,
- n-pentane vs 2-methylbutane for ISO-I2,
- 1-butanol vs 1-pentanol for ISO-I3,
- cis-2-pentene vs trans-2-pentene for ISO-I4,
- anti/gauche pentane central-bond conformations for ISO-I5,
- ethanol vs dimethyl ether for ISO-I6 only if not used in Watch; because Watch already uses them, ISO-I6 instead uses **propanal vs acetone/propanone** (both C3H6O),
- a held-out same-connectivity redraw with misleading page orientation for ISO-I7,
- mixed 4-pair bank ISO-I8.

## ISO-I1 - same molecule redraw

Two held-out redraws of **2-methylpentane**.

Required:
1. same formula,
2. same carbon neighbor connectivity,
3. only drawing orientation/path presentation differs,
4. classification same molecule.

Wrong:
> "Constitutional isomers because one drawing bends the chain differently."

Why fails: page geometry is mistaken for connectivity change.

---

## ISO-I2 - constitutional isomers

Use **n-pentane** vs **2-methylbutane**.

Required:
- both C5H12,
- different carbon connectivity/branching,
- constitutional isomers.

Wrong:
> "Same molecule because both have five carbons and twelve hydrogens."

Why fails: formula equality is necessary but not sufficient for identity.

---

## ISO-I3 - none from formula

Use **1-butanol** C4H10O vs **1-pentanol** C5H12O.

Required:
- formulas differ,
- therefore not isomers of each other,
- classification none.

Wrong:
> "Constitutional isomers because both are alcohols but have different carbon chains."

Why fails: functional-group similarity does not override different molecular formulas.

---

## ISO-I4 - stereoisomers

Use **cis-2-pentene** vs **trans-2-pentene**.

Required:
1. same formula,
2. same connectivity,
3. differing cis/trans spatial arrangement across the C=C,
4. not interconverted by simple page redraw/ordinary free single-bond rotation,
5. stereoisomers.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete:
> "Constitutional isomers because cis and trans put the carbon groups on different sides, so the atoms are connected differently."

Why fails: spatial relation changed, connectivity did not.

---

## ISO-I5 - conformers are same

Use two held-out conformations of **pentane** about a specified central C-C single bond, one staggered arrangement rotated relative to the other.

Required:
- same formula,
- same connectivity,
- ordinary C-C single-bond rotation interconverts,
- same molecule/different conformations.

Wrong:
> "Stereoisomers because the groups occupy different 3D positions."

Why fails: not every 3D difference is configurational stereoisomerism; this is conformational rotation.

---

## ISO-I6 - same formula, different functional connectivity

Use **propanal** `CH3CH2CHO` and **propanone (acetone)** `CH3COCH3`.

Both formula C3H6O.

Required:
- formula same,
- carbonyl exists in both,
- connectivity differs: terminal aldehyde connectivity versus internal ketone connectivity,
- constitutional isomers.

Scope note: learner need not master aldehyde nomenclature to classify connectivity; structures are fully drawn and the decisive neighbor pattern is visible.

Wrong:
> "Same molecule because both have three carbons, six hydrogens, one oxygen, and one C=O."

Why fails: local connectivity around the carbonyl differs.

---

## ISO-I7 - misleading page orientation

Use one held-out acyclic alkane drawn once normally and once as a mirrored/rotated bond-line redraw without any stereochemical descriptor or connectivity change.

Required:
- map same neighbor relationships,
- no bonds broken/reconnected,
- no fixed stereochemical difference introduced,
- same molecule.

Wrong:
> "Stereoisomers because the second drawing is a mirror image on the page."

Why fails: page mirroring alone does not establish a stereochemical distinction.

---

## ISO-I8 - full four-category bank

Present four independent fresh pairs:
A. same molecule: two redraws of **3-ethylhexane**,
B. constitutional: **hexane** vs **2-methylpentane** (C6H14),
C. stereoisomers: **cis-3-hexene** vs **trans-3-hexene**,
D. none: **hexane** C6H14 vs **1-hexene** C6H12.

Learner must classify each and give one decisive reason.

Scoring contract:
- A requires same formula + same connectivity + redraw only,
- B requires same formula + different connectivity,
- C requires same formula + same connectivity + fixed cis/trans spatial difference,
- D requires formula mismatch,
- all four labels and reasons must align.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete:
> "A stereoisomers because drawings differ; B same because formulas match; C constitutional because cis/trans changes sides; D constitutional because both have six carbons."

Why fails: each decision uses the wrong level of evidence.

Contradiction rule:
A correct label paired with a contradictory reason does not earn explanation evidence.

# 12. INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE RULE

Shared mastery requires:
1. cold scaffold-0 classification,
2. role-preserving explanation using formula/connectivity/spatial evidence,
3. second different cold item after meaningful interval.

A correct category guessed from appearance is not explanation evidence.

Support/atom mapping/rotation reveal contaminates current item.

---

# 13. EXPLAIN-WHY

E-W1: Why can two different drawings be same molecule? -> same atom connectivity; page/conformation can change.

E-W2: Why does same formula not prove same molecule? -> constitutional isomers share formula but differ connectivity.

E-W3: Why are cis/trans alkenes not constitutional isomers? -> connectivity same; fixed spatial relation differs.

E-W4: Why are ordinary single-bond conformers not separate stereoisomers in this classification? -> ordinary rotation interconverts without connectivity/configuration change.

E-W5: Why does formula mismatch end the isomer question? -> isomers require same atom inventory.

---

# 14. TRANSFER

1. Diagnose a classmate who calls two rotated drawings constitutional isomers.
2. Given two same-formula structures, highlight the first connectivity mismatch.
3. Given same connectivity, decide conformer versus stereoisomer using ordinary bond-rotation test.
4. Connect to chairs: a chair flip preserving up/down is same molecule, not a new stereoisomer.

---

# 15. TEST-OUT / LATER RETRIEVAL

3-item cold probe:
- redraw vs same,
- constitutional pair,
- stereoisomer or none pair.

3/3 with valid reasons -> skip full teaching; collect later evidence.
2/3 -> target failed decision layer.
0-1/3 -> full lesson.

Later retrieval uses a fresh pair from a different representation.

# 18. RELEASE GATE

Block production unless:
1. formula gate precedes isomer classification,
2. connectivity is explicitly mapped,
3. conformers are distinguished from stereoisomers,
4. cis/trans example is chemically valid,
5. page rotation/redraw is not treated as connectivity change,
6. same formula is not treated as sufficient for identity/constitutional classification,
7. formula mismatch produces none,
8. Build starts from unclassified pair,
9. Guided/cold examples are distinct,
10. cold contracts reject keyword-complete reversals,
11. objective/explanation evidence separate,
12. support contamination forces fresh evidence,
13. scope does not pretend to teach full stereochemistry,
14. accessibility/mobile requirements hold.

# 19. DEFINITION OF DONE

> **She can compare formula, connectivity, and real spatial arrangement in that order and explain why a pair is the same molecule, constitutional isomers, stereoisomers, or none, without classifying by how different the drawings look.**
