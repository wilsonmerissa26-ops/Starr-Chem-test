# UNIT 1 LESSON U1-06 - IR SPECTROSCOPY AS BOND-VIBRATION EVIDENCE
## Fully authored beginner lesson script

**Status:** content draft for external chemistry/instructional audit before runtime implementation.

**Parent curriculum:** `UNIT1_BEGINNER_TEACHING_CURRICULUM.md`

**Teaching-engine contract:** `DR_MERISSA_TEACHING_ENGINE_SPEC.md`

**Frozen template standards:** U1-00, U1-01, U1-02, U1-03, U1-04, U1-05, U1-07, U1-08, U1-09, U1-10, U1-11, U1-12, U1-13.

**Purpose:** teach a beginner to use an IR spectrum as evidence about bond types and functional groups rather than as a picture to memorize. The learner must understand the historical course spectrum convention, connect broad/sharp absorptions to structural possibilities, distinguish presence evidence from absence evidence, and match unfamiliar structures to spectra by ruling candidates in and out.

This lesson intentionally separates **course evidence** from **general reference chemistry**. The Fall 2026 syllabus confirms Lab 1 includes IR but does not provide a course-specific absorption table. The Fall 2025 Test 1 is used only as a historical style/coverage signal. Numerical ranges in this file are therefore labeled as external reference values, not Mercer-specific promises.

Cold items carry role-preserving scoring contracts from the start.

# 1. VERIFIED SOURCE BASIS

## A. Current-course evidence

The Fall 2026 CHM 221 syllabus confirms:
- Lab 1 is **IR, Functional Groups, and Molecular Models**,
- the week of Aug 24 also covers Chapter 4,
- Test 1 follows the next week,
- tests are cumulative, closed book, and closed notes.

The syllabus does **not** provide an IR absorption table or exact peak ranges.

## B. Historical course-style evidence

The Fall 2025 Test 1 included a three-way IR matching problem with:
- percent transmittance on the y-axis,
- wavenumber decreasing from roughly 3500 toward 500 cm^-1 from left to right,
- candidate structures representing a ketone, a carboxylic acid, and an ether.

This is a style/coverage signal only, not a guarantee that Fall 2026 will repeat those exact molecules or the exact plotting convention.

## C. External reference chemistry used for production

The following broad values are standard reference ranges and must be labeled in content data as **reference values, not Mercer-specific values**:

- alcohol O-H stretch: approximately **3400-3650 cm^-1**, strong/broad in common hydrogen-bonded examples,
- carbonyl C=O: approximately **1670-1780 cm^-1**, usually strong,
- ketone C=O: commonly near **1715 cm^-1**,
- carboxylic-acid O-H: approximately **2500-3300 cm^-1**, very broad,
- carboxylic-acid C=O: commonly near the low-1700 cm^-1 region,
- ether/alcohol C-O: commonly in the **~1050-1150 cm^-1** neighborhood for simple alcohol/ether examples, but the fingerprint region is crowded and a single C-O peak must not be treated as a stand-alone identity proof.

The lesson teaches diagnostic **patterns and combinations** first. Exact numbers are secondary anchors.

# 2. LESSON GOAL

A beginner should leave able to:

1. explain that IR measures absorption associated with molecular bond vibrations,
2. distinguish a spectrum from a literal drawing of a molecule,
3. read the historical course-style x-axis as wavenumber in cm^-1 decreasing left to right,
4. read percent-transmittance spectra correctly: stronger absorption appears as a deeper downward feature,
5. understand that an absorbance-style spectrum would point upward instead and that axis labels, not memorized orientation, decide the convention,
6. distinguish broad versus relatively sharp features qualitatively,
7. recognize a broad alcohol O-H region as evidence for an alcohol-type O-H bond,
8. recognize the very broad carboxylic-acid O-H pattern plus a carbonyl as a stronger two-feature signature,
9. recognize a strong carbonyl-region absorption as evidence for a C=O-containing candidate,
10. recognize that a simple ether has no O-H donor stretch and no carbonyl stretch,
11. use fingerprint-region C-O evidence only as supporting evidence, not as a one-peak identity shortcut,
12. match a small candidate set to spectra by combining presence and absence evidence,
13. explain why one candidate is ruled in and another ruled out,
14. solve a fresh Test-1-style matching task without labels or hints.

The lesson is not complete because the learner memorizes `OH = broad` and `C=O = 1700`. It is complete when she can inspect the whole diagnostic region, use the actual axis convention, and make a defensible structure-spectrum match.

# 4. LEARNER-FACING ORIENTATION

## Screen title

**IR spectra: bond vibrations leave clues**

Dr. Merissa:

> "An IR spectrum is evidence about bonds in a molecule. Certain bonds absorb infrared energy when they vibrate. We are not going to memorize every dip on the graph. We are going to learn which big clues matter and how to use them together."

### Immediate visual

Show two balls connected by a spring.

Animate:
- stretching apart,
- compressing together.

Then replace the spring model with a simple O-H bond.

Dr. Merissa:

> "The spring is only a model. Real chemical bonds do not behave exactly like metal springs, but the stretch helps us picture why different bonds can absorb different IR energies."

### Prediction

**If two bond types vibrate differently, would you expect every bond type to absorb in exactly the same place?**

Correct: **No**.

Orientation is instructional only.

# 6. TEACH THE SPECTRUM CONVENTION BEFORE PEAK RANGES

## WATCH 1 - x-axis

Use a historical-course-style blank spectrum.

Show labels approximately:
`3500 | 2500 | 1500 | 500 cm^-1`
from left to right.

Dr. Merissa:

> "In the historical course spectrum style, wavenumber gets smaller as you move to the right. That can feel backwards if you expect every graph to increase left to right. Read the numbers; do not guess."

Interaction:
**Which side has the higher wavenumber, left or right?**

Correct: **left**.

## WATCH 2 - y-axis in percent transmittance

Show a flat high-transmittance baseline, then a downward trough.

Dr. Merissa:

> "On a percent-transmittance spectrum, a bond absorbing IR means less light is transmitted at that wavenumber. So the absorption appears as a downward dip."

Prediction:
**On this percent-transmittance graph, would a strong absorption generally make a deeper dip or a higher spike?**

Correct: **deeper dip**.

### Critical convention guard

Then show a tiny alternate graph labeled `Absorbance`.

> "Some sources plot absorbance instead, where absorptions point upward. The chemistry did not reverse. The plotting convention changed. Always read the y-axis."

Cold evidence must test convention reading, not memorization of `IR peaks go down`.

# 7. BUILD MEANING BEFORE NUMBERS

## WATCH 3 - broad versus relatively sharp

Show two schematic absorptions:
- a narrow strong carbonyl-like dip,
- a broad O-H-like depression.

Do not label the functional groups initially.

Prompt:
**Do these features have the same shape?**

Correct: No.

Dr. Merissa:

> "Where a feature occurs matters, but shape also matters. A broad O-H absorption can cover a wide range. A carbonyl absorption is usually much narrower."

Do not teach exact linewidth thresholds.

# 8. I DO - DIAGNOSTIC PATTERNS

## WATCH 4 - alcohol O-H

### Supported molecule
Use **2-propanol**.

Highlight only the O-H bond first.

Show a schematic percent-transmittance spectrum with a broad strong absorption in the high-wavenumber O-H region.

Dr. Merissa:

> "An alcohol O-H bond commonly gives a strong broad absorption in roughly the mid-3000 cm^-1 region. A useful reference range is about 3400-3650 cm^-1, with hydrogen bonding often making the band broader and shifting it lower within that general region."

Then show the C-O region only as secondary support.

Guardrail:
> "Do not call a molecule an alcohol from one random fingerprint-region dip. The broad O-H feature is the more diagnostic clue here."

Prediction:
**If a candidate structure has no O-H bond at all, should a broad alcohol O-H feature support that candidate?**

Correct: No.

---

## WATCH 5 - ketone carbonyl

### Supported molecule
Use **3-pentanone**.

Highlight `C=O`.

Show a strong relatively sharp dip in the carbonyl region near the low 1700s cm^-1.

Dr. Merissa:

> "A C=O bond produces a strong absorption in the carbonyl region. A broad reference window for carbonyl compounds is about 1670-1780 cm^-1; a simple saturated ketone is commonly near 1715 cm^-1."

Then:
> "The important beginner clue is not `1715 exactly`. It is `strong carbonyl-region evidence` plus the structure of the candidate."

Prompt:
**Would this carbonyl clue fit an ether that has no C=O bond?**

Correct: No.

---

## WATCH 6 - carboxylic acid requires a combination

### Supported molecule
Use **propanoic acid**.

Show:
- a very broad O-H feature extending roughly through 2500-3300 cm^-1,
- a strong carbonyl feature in the low-1700 region.

Dr. Merissa:

> "A carboxylic acid carries both pieces in the same functional group: O-H and C=O. Its O-H absorption is characteristically very broad and can spread down into the 2500-3300 cm^-1 region, while the carbonyl gives a strong C=O absorption."

Prediction:
**Which is stronger evidence for a carboxylic acid: one carbonyl alone, or the combination of a carbonyl plus the very broad acid O-H pattern?**

Correct: **the combination**.

Guardrail:
A ketone also has C=O, so `carbonyl present` alone cannot prove carboxylic acid.

---

## WATCH 7 - ether by exclusion plus supporting C-O

### Supported molecule
Use **ethoxyethane (diethyl ether)**.

Show:
- no broad O-H diagnostic band,
- no carbonyl-region C=O band,
- strong C-O-region activity in the fingerprint region as supporting evidence.

Dr. Merissa:

> "A simple ether has a C-O-C bond but no O-H and no C=O. In a small candidate set, the absence of the O-H and carbonyl signatures can be powerful evidence. The C-O absorption in the fingerprint region can support the match, but that region is crowded, so we do not identify an ether from one fingerprint dip alone."

Prompt:
**If a candidate spectrum has a strong carbonyl dip, can a simple ether with no C=O be the best match?**

Correct: No.

---

## WATCH 8 - three-way structure-spectrum match

Use three supported candidates that are not cold-reserved:
- **2-butanone**,
- **butanoic acid**,
- **methyl propyl ether (1-methoxypropane)**.

Provide three clean generated spectra, not copied historical exam images.

Dr. Merissa models this order:
1. Find the very broad acid O-H + C=O combination -> acid.
2. Find strong C=O without broad O-H -> ketone.
3. Remaining spectrum lacks both diagnostic features and has supporting C-O fingerprint evidence -> ether.

Narration:
> "Notice I did not start by staring at every wiggle. I started with the most diagnostic clues and used elimination."

- up in absorbance,

# 11. MISCONCEPTION CONTRASTS

## M1 - "Every downward dip is a different functional group"

Repair: show multiple fingerprint dips in a hydrocarbon-like spectrum and contrast with one diagnostic region. Teach that the full spectrum contains many vibrations.

## M2 - "IR always points down"

Repair: show %T versus absorbance conventions side by side. Axis label decides direction.

## M3 - "The x-axis increases left to right because all graphs do"

Repair: make learner read printed wavenumber values from the historical style.

## M4 - "Any oxygen means a broad O-H band"

Repair: alcohol versus ether direct-connectivity contrast from U1-03.

## M5 - "Any carbonyl means carboxylic acid"

Repair: ketone carbonyl alone versus acid carbonyl + very broad O-H combination.

## M6 - "No exact 1715 peak means no carbonyl"

Repair: show broad carbonyl reference window and explain structural/environmental shifts. Exact position is evidence, not a magic single number.

## M7 - "One C-O fingerprint peak proves ether"

Repair: fingerprint region is crowded; require absence of O-H/C=O plus supporting C-O and candidate context.

## M8 - "Broad means stronger than sharp"

Repair: width and intensity are different properties. A band can be broad without `broad = strongest` being a valid rule.

## M9 - "If two spectra share one peak, they are the same molecule"

Repair: one shared bond can create one shared region. Whole diagnostic pattern and candidate structures matter.

## M10 - "Absence evidence never matters"

Repair: if a candidate requires C=O and the spectrum lacks carbonyl evidence, that absence can rule the candidate out in an appropriate clean problem.

## IR-I1 - read the graph before chemistry

Show an unlabeled schematic absorption on a graph with:
- y-axis `% Transmittance`,
- x-axis `Wavenumber (cm^-1)`,
- x labels decreasing left to right.

Prompt:
**Which side is higher wavenumber, and does the absorption appear upward or downward on this graph? Explain from the axis labels.**

Required:
1. higher wavenumber is on the left,
2. % transmittance means stronger absorption appears as a downward dip,
3. answer is based on axis labels/convention, not a universal `IR peaks go down` rule.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete:
> "Higher wavenumber is on the right and the peak goes down because IR always points down."

Why it fails: reverses x-axis and treats one plotting convention as universal.

Contradiction rule:
Correct `downward` paired with `because absorbance gets smaller` does not earn explanation evidence on a %T plot.

---

## IR-I2 - alcohol versus ether

Use two structures:
- **3-pentanol**,
- **1-ethoxypropane**.

Provide one spectrum with a broad alcohol O-H feature and one without it.

Prompt:
**Match each structure and explain the decisive bond evidence.**

Required:
1. both contain oxygen,
2. only 3-pentanol has O-H,
3. broad O-H-region absorption supports the alcohol,
4. ether lacks an O-H donor bond and therefore should not be assigned the broad alcohol O-H feature,
5. C-O fingerprint evidence may support but may not replace the O-H reasoning.

Wrong:
> "Either could match the broad band because both contain oxygen."

Why fails: element presence is substituted for bond connectivity.

---

## IR-I3 - ketone carbonyl

Use **2-hexanone**.

Prompt:
**Which diagnostic absorption must a spectrum have to support this ketone, and what structural bond causes it?**

Required:
1. strong carbonyl-region evidence,
2. caused by C=O,
3. simple ketone commonly near low 1700s/reference around 1715 cm^-1,
4. exact 1715 is not required as a single magic coordinate.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete:
> "A broad O-H band around 3400 proves the ketone because ketones contain oxygen."

Why fails: wrong bond and wrong feature.

---

## IR-I4 - carboxylic acid combination

Use **2-methylbutanoic acid**.

Provide a generated spectrum with:
- very broad acid O-H region,
- strong carbonyl-region feature.

Prompt:
**Why is the combination stronger evidence for a carboxylic acid than the carbonyl alone?**

Required:
1. acid contains both O-H and C=O,
2. acid O-H is very broad and commonly spans roughly 2500-3300 cm^-1,
3. carbonyl gives strong low-1700-region evidence,
4. ketones also contain C=O, so carbonyl alone is not unique to acids.

Wrong:
> "The acid is identified by C=O only; the broad O-H is unrelated."

Why fails: discards the distinguishing second feature.

---

## IR-I5 - ether by structure + absence evidence

Use **methyl tert-butyl ether**.

Provide a generated spectrum with no broad O-H and no carbonyl, plus fingerprint C-O activity.

Prompt:
**Explain why this spectrum can support the ether and why a strong carbonyl feature would rule it out.**

Required:
1. ether contains C-O-C,
2. no O-H bond -> no alcohol/acid O-H diagnostic requirement,
3. no C=O -> strong carbonyl feature would contradict the structure,
4. fingerprint C-O is supporting evidence, not sole proof.

Wrong:
> "A sharp feature anywhere near 1100 proves ether even if there is a strong C=O peak."

Why fails: treats crowded fingerprint evidence as overriding incompatible carbonyl evidence.

---

## IR-I6 - historical-style three-way match

Candidates:
- **3-heptanone**,
- **hexanoic acid**,
- **1-ethoxybutane**.

Generate three fresh %T spectra in the historical visual convention.

Required mapping logic:
1. acid spectrum = very broad acid O-H + strong C=O,
2. ketone spectrum = strong C=O without broad acid O-H,
3. ether spectrum = lacks both broad O-H and carbonyl, with supporting C-O fingerprint region,
4. learner explains at least one exclusion per spectrum.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete:
> "The ketone and acid can be swapped because both contain C=O; the ether is whichever spectrum has the most fingerprint peaks."

Why fails: ignores the acid O-H discriminator and treats peak count as identity evidence.

## IR-I7 - elimination from a required bond

Use **2-methyl-2-butanol** as the only structure.

Show three fresh candidate spectra:
A. broad alcohol O-H, no C=O,
B. strong C=O, no broad O-H,
C. neither O-H nor C=O.

Prompt:
**Choose the best spectrum and reject the other two using structure.**

Required:
1. molecule has O-H,
2. molecule has no C=O,
3. A best fits,
4. B contradicted by carbonyl requirement and missing broad O-H,
5. C contradicted by missing O-H evidence.

Wrong:
> "B is best because any oxygen-containing molecule should have a strong peak near 1700."

Why fails: falsely equates oxygen presence with carbonyl.

## IR-I8 - full mixed matching

Candidates:
- **4-octanone**,
- **heptanoic acid**,
- **3-hexanol**,
- **1-methoxypentane**.

Provide four fresh generated spectra.

Required:
1. acid -> very broad acid O-H + C=O,
2. ketone -> C=O without broad O-H,
3. alcohol -> broad alcohol O-H without C=O,
4. ether -> neither broad O-H nor C=O, with supporting C-O fingerprint evidence,
5. all four matches use bond-based reasoning,
6. at least two explicit exclusions are chemically valid.

Wrong-but-keyword-complete:
> "All four contain oxygen, so the main difference is whichever has the biggest peak."

Why fails: ignores exact bond patterns, axis convention, and diagnostic regions.

Second failure mode:
> "Acid and alcohol are interchangeable because both have O-H; ketone and ether are interchangeable because neither has an O-H donor."

Why fails: ignores carbonyl evidence that separates acid from alcohol and ketone from ether.

Contradiction rule:
Correct final matching with explanations that assign C=O to the ether or deny C=O in the ketone does not earn clean explanation evidence.

# 15. EXPLAIN-WHY CONTRACTS

## E-W1 - Why does absorption point down here?
Required: %T decreases where radiation is absorbed; convention-specific, not universal.

## E-W2 - Why does a broad O-H region support alcohol?
Required: structure has O-H; O-H stretch appears broadly in reference high-wavenumber region; oxygen alone is not enough.

## E-W3 - Why is a carbonyl peak not enough to prove carboxylic acid?
Required: ketones and other carbonyl compounds also have C=O; acid needs additional O-H evidence.

## E-W4 - Why can absence evidence rule out a candidate?
Required: if structure requires a bond such as C=O and a clean spectrum lacks expected strong carbonyl evidence, candidate becomes inconsistent in the intended introductory problem.

## E-W5 - Why not identify ether from one C-O fingerprint peak?
Required: fingerprint region is crowded; C-O is supporting evidence and must agree with absence of O-H/C=O and candidate structure.

Every explanation rejects role-reversed versions.

# 21. DEFINITION OF DONE

> **She can read the actual IR graph convention, identify the strongest diagnostic bond evidence, use presence and absence together, and match an unfamiliar structure to a spectrum without treating every dip or every oxygen atom as a memorized shortcut.**
